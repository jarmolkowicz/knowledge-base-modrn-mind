# Motivation through the design of work: Test of a theory

## Citation

Hackman, J. R., & Oldham, G. R. (1976). Motivation through the design of work: Test of a theory. Organizational Behavior and Human Performance, 16, 250–279.

Supplied 30-page published PDF; printed pages250–279. PDF locators used below; author initials avoid garbled given-name extraction.

## Type

Observational field test with 658 employees, 62 jobs and seven organizations; employee questionnaires, observer ratings, supervisor performance ratings and absence records.

## Key Insight

Work design should separate what a job requires from what the worker experiences and what outcomes follow. The study supports parts of that model, not every causal link or its numerical scoring formula.

## Key Findings

- The model proposes skill variety, task identity and task significance as sources of meaningfulness; autonomy as a source of responsibility; and feedback from the job as a source of knowledge of results. (PDF pp.6–10)
- Associations with motivation and satisfaction were stronger than with performance and absenteeism. Common-method variance and differences in recording behavioral outcomes limit interpretation. (PDF pp.12–23)
- Responsibility was associated with more than autonomy, and autonomy retained relationships with outcomes beyond its proposed mediator. The hypothesized one-to-one mapping was therefore imperfect. (PDF pp.17–24)
- Growth-need moderation appeared for several relationships, but not absenteeism. Even lower-growth-need groups generally showed positive relationships; the results do not warrant excluding them from enriched work. (PDF pp.20–26)
- The Motivating Potential Score was not more adequate than simpler alternatives. Practical usefulness had not yet been tested in an actual change project in this study. (PDF pp.24–28)

## Key Passages

> "The results do not meaningfully differentiate among the models."
> — Hackman and Oldham (1976), PDF p.24

> "It offers no explicit guidance for the effective design of work for interacting teams"
> — Hackman and Oldham (1976), PDF p.28

## Relevance

A foundation for analyzing AI-related job changes without treating task removal or added discretion as sufficient evidence of preserved [[agency]].

## Contradicts / Extends

Earlier work-design framework complements contemporary meaning and AI-adoption entries. Does not measure current AI effects or establish universal benefits from complexity.

## Limitations

Nonexperimental design, common-method concerns for attitude measures, incomplete outcomes in some jobs, non-comparable performance standards and imperfect absence coding. Job autonomy is not identical to SDT volition. Limited guidance for interdependent teams.
