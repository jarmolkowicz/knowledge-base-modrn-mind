# Corgnet, Fumagalli & Rezaei (2026) — Motivation with an AI Coworker

## Citation

Corgnet, B., Fumagalli, E., & Rezaei, S. (2026). *Beyond Performance: Replacing a Human Coworker with AI Enhances Motivation*. Working paper dated June 12, 2026. AEA preregistration: AEARCTR-0016059. No journal publication is identified in the supplied manuscript.

## Type

Paper — preregistered online randomized experiment, with psychological measures, a behavioral task-choice measure, and independent output ratings.

## Key Insight

In a short creative task, people paired with ChatGPT-5.2 reported more satisfaction, interest, autonomy, competence, and relatedness than people paired with another newly recruited participant. Their outputs also rated higher. However, the groups did not differ meaningfully in willingness to repeat the task alone: positive reports should not be described as demonstrated sustained motivation.

## Evidence

The study analyzed 1,091 US Prolific participants: 698 assigned to human–human teams and 393 to human–AI teams. The larger human condition accommodated paired observations. Participants proposed a book title, genre description, and background-image description, communicating through a shared interface; the first 90 seconds required chat before answer fields opened. The creative output itself earned a fixed payment independent of quality. Sessions averaged about 40 minutes, including about 15 minutes on collaboration (pp. 8–11).

Human matching had real frictions: 218 unmatched participants exited, and 58 participants with non-completing partners remained in analysis. The manuscript reports balance on measured covariates and robustness to removing the latter group. This is a comparison with newly formed online pairs, not established coworkers or solo work.

Table B.5 (p. 59) gives self-report effects: job interest d = 0.182, satisfaction d = 0.323, relatedness d = 0.189, autonomy d = 0.480, and competence d = 0.138. Overall output quality averaged 6.08 versus 5.67 out of 10; d = 0.33. Ratings came from 180 separate raters, blind to condition; the overall quality model used 983 observations (pp. 11–12, 21–22).

The behavioral measure asked participants to choose a five-minute solo repeat versus clicking tasks of varying duration. Table B.3 shows almost identical average preference scores: 4.66 versus 4.63 (p. 57). SEM indirect paths through psychological needs and team climate were statistically significant, but these mediators were not randomized. LLM-inferred chat scores are an additional model-based analysis, not independent measurement of participants' mental states.

## Relevance

Useful counterevidence to universal motivational-harm claims. It shows that comparator, task, and team history matter. Short-term felt connection with AI also cannot establish preservation of workplace relationships or long-term human [[agency]].

## Supports

- [[basic-psychological-needs]] — satisfaction and frustration of autonomy, competence and relatedness; not a measure of retained skill.

- [[agency]] — qualifies claims that AI presence always lowers perceived control.
- [[human-ai-complementarity]] — human–AI beat human–human here, but there is no AI-alone arm establishing strict synergy.

## Contradicts / Extends

- Qualifies: [[wu-collaboration-motivation-2025]] — positive reports versus an unfamiliar human collaborator differ from Wu's solo-work and transition comparisons; this is not a direct failed replication.
- Qualifies: [[affective-trust-deficit]] — higher reported relatedness is possible, but relatedness and affective trust are different measures.
- Qualifies: [[hermann-genai-psychology-work-2025]] — basic psychological needs can be supported under some arrangements rather than necessarily frustrated.

## Open Questions

- Would the finding survive replacing a familiar colleague, repeating work for months, or using a less agreeable system?
- Does improved self-report eventually produce greater voluntary engagement? The immediate behavioral comparison did not show this.
- Matching attrition, unequal partner availability, model behavior, and short paid tasks limit workplace interpretation.
- Preregistration is reported in the manuscript; the registry and publication status have not been checked externally during this distillation.
