# Turn creative disagreement into your own checklist

## Use When

Repeated feedback conversations reveal recurring choices in your writing, music or other craft, and you want something you can use without reopening AI.

## Try It

1. Bring work you have already begun and ask for a critical reading of a specific choice.
2. Argue with or decline comments that do not fit the work. Try revisions yourself and judge them in the medium: sing a lyric, read a line aloud, or inspect the composition.
3. After several decisions, name the principles you actually used. AI may help summarize the discussion; you select and rewrite the rules.
4. Apply the short checklist to another piece without AI. Change a rule when it fails the new work. The explicit revision of rules is an editorial safeguard.

## Origin

Joshua Sherman (First Radio On), in Sam Illingworth and co-editor Alyssa Fu Ward’s “How Creatives Are Actually Using AI,” 17 March 2026, L194–208, especially L206. Editorial label and operational elaboration of his described practice.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis / observed or reported:** Sherman narrates two songwriting exchanges, rejects some feedback and describes a personal checklist that he applies without AI. He reports the decisions; no independent quality or transfer test is provided.

**Related research:** Related to [[think-first]] in preserving human material and choice. No direct empirical evaluation of the checklist routine reviewed.

**Untested:** Whether the checklist supports better work or lasting craft development. Applying a rule does not by itself show expertise.

## Limits

Keep rules provisional and personal. A model-generated summary can misstate why you chose something. Avoid turning one song’s successful choice into a formula that flattens later work. Hearing is not required; use the evaluative mode available for the craft.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Can you use, explain and sometimes reject a checklist item without consulting the conversation?
