# Ask how to find out before asking AI

## Use When

A child asks a factual question and reaching for AI would end the inquiry immediately.

Author’s intended benefit: Keep the child involved in choosing an inquiry route.

## Try It

1. Ask the child how you could find out before opening AI.
2. Consider a feasible observation, simple experiment, book or person to ask.
3. Pursue an appropriate route together. Joel Salinas (474) describes consulting a household encyclopedia first and using supervised AI for further questions.
4. If AI is useful afterward, compare its answer with what you found. [Inference] Keep unresolved disagreement visible rather than treating either the child’s confidence or AI’s fluency as proof.
5. Kunal Shah’s proposed variant (714) asks a why question at a meal and returns to the child’s inquiry the next day.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** First-person family routine.

**Observed or reported:** Describes replacing an immediate request to Claude with a question about how to find out; no learning assessment.

**Related research:** Bastani and colleagues distinguish assisted practice from later unaided mathematics performance. That school study does not evaluate these family conversations or justify an age-general rule. See [[bastani-guardrails-math-rct-2025]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Confidence in a child’s answer is not accuracy; the coral anecdote demonstrates stance, not verification.
- The guest leads an AI product organization.
- No age-specific outcome or general developmental benefit established.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice whether the child can explain a way to investigate another question. An enjoyable exchange is not evidence of lasting learning.
