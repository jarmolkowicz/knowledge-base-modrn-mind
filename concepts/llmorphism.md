# LLMorphism

## What It Is

Capraro's proposed name for the biased belief that human cognition works like a large language model. The problematic inference is that similar language outputs imply similar underlying thought processes. The construct has no validated measure or direct empirical evidence of prevalence or effects in the supplied manuscript.

## Why It Matters

[Speculation] If people treat humans mainly as generators of plausible text, they may overlook experience, embodied needs, [[judgment]], and accountability when assessing human work. Capraro proposes these consequences; the paper does not demonstrate them.

## Key Insight

The direction differs from [[anthropomorphism-of-technology]]: human qualities are attributed to machines in anthropomorphism; model-like properties are projected onto human thought in LLMorphism. The latter is narrower than general machine metaphors and does not necessarily involve hostility or denying human worth.

Some comparisons remain useful: humans also predict, learn patterns, and recombine ideas. The proposed bias begins when partial resemblance becomes a general account of human cognition. Mechanisms, moderators, and possible social effects remain hypotheses.
