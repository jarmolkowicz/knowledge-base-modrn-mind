## Proposed Replacements

These replace the destination's broad framing; do not append them beneath contradictory claims. Keep status emerging. Preserve the separately scoped Raj, Frenzel-Piasentin and Zhu findings except for the explicit changes below. The earlier papers' original data were not re-audited in this revision.

### Replace "What It Is"

Perceived authenticity is the judgment that work reflects its creator's own thought, effort and voice. In some experiments, identical writing was judged less authentic when attributed to AI use. This is an observer's perception, not proof of actual insincerity or a rule that every form of assistance lowers authenticity.

### Replace "Why It Matters"

Perceived authenticity is one proposed explanation for the [[disclosure-penalty]]. Studies link it to evaluations of AI-attributed work, but measured mediator relationships alone do not establish a universal causal mechanism. Task, production process and the outcome being rated matter; work ratings are not the same as observed career or relationship consequences.

### In "Key Insight": replace the opening sentence of the Raj paragraph

Raj et al. (2026) reported statistical mediation through perceived authenticity across their creative-writing experiments (16 experiments, N = 27,491).

Retain the remainder of that paragraph as a report of the tested creative-writing settings and mitigation attempts, not proof that every possible intervention fails.

### In "Key Insight": replace the paragraph beginning "This suggests authenticity perceptions are not a knowledge gap"

Persistence in those experiments does not establish an immutable evaluative reflex or show that learning and experience can never change these judgments.

Claessens et al. (2026) found that identical writing attributed to AI assistance or full outsourcing was judged less authentic and meaningful than writing attributed to an unaided person (Study 3). In a separate love-letter study, perceived authenticity and care were associated with character evaluations in a path model; this does not prove the causal ordering of those perceptions. A personalized-model manipulation produced no clear advantage over generic AI, not proof of exact equivalence. These findings concern perceived authenticity, not actual sincerity.
