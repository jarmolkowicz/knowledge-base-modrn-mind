# Upskilling-Deskilling Paradox

## Evidence Limits

The trajectories below are proposals from SCAN. Its Shukla and Krook references are identified in Sources, but neither has a separate canonical source entry here. Their citation does not validate the trajectories as measures of learning or skill loss.

## What It Is

The same AI tools and practices can lead to both skill development (upskilling) AND skill erosion (deskilling), depending on how they're used and the user's starting knowledge level.

## Why It Matters

This paradox explains why advice about AI use can seem contradictory. "Use AI to learn faster" and "AI is making you less capable" can both be true—for different people, tasks, or usage patterns.

## Key Insight

SCAN proposes the following learning trajectories. They are not validated measures of skill change; independent performance needs assessment:

**Upskilling path:** S → A → C
- Tasks that start in Substitute zone (AI does it) migrate to Aid (AI helps you do it) then to Complement (you do it, AI assists)
- SCAN proposes deliberate practice, reflection and explain-back exercises to support this path

**Deskilling path:** C → A → S
- Tasks you could do independently slip to needing AI assistance, then full delegation
- SCAN proposes that repeated delegation without practice could contribute to this path

[Inference] Task design and practice may influence the direction of learning, but zone labels or choices alone do not establish it.
