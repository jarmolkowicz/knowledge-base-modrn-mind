# Allan et al. (2019) — Meaningful Work and Its Correlates

## Citation

Allan, B. A., Batz-Barbarich, C., Sterling, H. M., & Tay, L. (2019). Outcomes of Meaningful Work: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Management Studies, 56(3). https://doi.org/10.1111/joms.12406

Published issue year 2019; copyright 2018. Supplied 29-page PDF; PDF-page locators.

## Type

Random-effects meta-analysis of 44 articles, 76 correlations and 23,144 participants, with reliability corrections and meta-analytic structural equation modeling.

## Key Insight

Meaningful work relates strongly to work attitudes, but is not interchangeable with satisfaction, engagement or performance. Associations do not prove that making work more meaningful causes those outcomes.

## Key Findings

- Included 39 journal articles and five dissertations. Independent samples were an eligibility criterion; the authors report zero exclusions for violating that criterion (PDF pp.8–9).
- Reliability-corrected correlations were stronger for engagement, commitment and job satisfaction than for self-rated performance and organizational citizenship behavior. Heterogeneity was substantial (Table I, p.12).
- A model placing meaningful work before attitudes and then distal outcomes fit better than the tested alternatives. It remains a model of correlations; the matrix also used earlier meta-analyses (pp.10–15).
- Scale-type comparisons were nonsignificant for the four outcomes tested; this does not establish equivalence of all meaning measures. Publication-bias checks were limited to outcome groups with at least ten studies (pp.10–13).

## Key Passages

> "meta-analytic correlation coefficients do not provide cause and effect"
> — Allan et al. (2019), PDF p.17

> "having meaningful work does not reflect a continuous psychological state"
> — Allan et al. (2019), PDF p.2

## Relevance

Provides a work-specific evidence base for [[meaningful-work]]. [Inference] An AI rollout should assess experienced significance separately from speed, satisfaction and self-rated performance.

## Supports

- [[meaningful-work]] — definition and aggregate associations.

## Contradicts / Extends

Complements [[callari-meaningful-work-2025]] with aggregate non-AI evidence and [[baumeister-happy-meaningful-life-2013]] with a work-specific focus. Neither comparison establishes an AI effect.

## Limitations

Correlational synthesis, mostly US and entirely Western settings. Several outcome groups have fewer than five studies. Meaningful-work definitions differ; the authors favor global judgments and treat multidimensional measures partly as experiences or processes. High correlations can reflect common-method overlap. Do not describe self-rated performance as objective output.

## Open Questions

- Which causal changes in job design improve meaning without intensifying work?
- How do global evaluations and momentary experiences respond differently to AI?
