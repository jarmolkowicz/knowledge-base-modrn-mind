# Ask children about AI before setting rules

## Use When

You are making a family or classroom AI rule without knowing what children already use or believe.

Author’s intended benefit: Hear children’s existing use and concerns before setting or revising rules.

## Try It

1. Ask what they think AI is for, what they use and what worries them; write down their responses.
2. Ask whom they could consult if AI tells them something important.
3. Discuss when a system might be guessing and who benefits from a convincing answer.
4. [Inference] Use what you heard to explain the rule and its reasons, including questions that remain open. Revisit the conversation as use changes.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Parent anecdote and proposed education-policy practices.

**Observed or reported:** Reports a child repeating an anti-AI position learned from other children; does not report outcomes of the five proposed practices.

**Related research:** Bastani and colleagues distinguish assisted practice from later unaided mathematics performance. That school study does not evaluate these family conversations or justify an age-general rule. See [[bastani-guardrails-math-rct-2025]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- The article’s legal and policy claims were not independently refreshed in this review.
- Listening does not replace adult responsibilities or tool assessment.
- The article itself recognizes uncertainty; comparisons with other prohibition policies are analogies, not causal AI evidence.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice whether the discussion reveals an assumption you had wrong. This is participation in rule-making, not evidence that every use should be allowed.
