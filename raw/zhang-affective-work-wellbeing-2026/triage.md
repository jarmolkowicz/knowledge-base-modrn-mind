## Summary
Zhang and colleagues use a three-wave, time-lagged survey of 360 employees in knowledge-intensive industries. Reported AI use predicts later affective work well-being through greater work meaningfulness and engagement.

Higher perceived human-AI collaboration quality strengthens both indirect paths. The study provides useful positive counterevidence, though all measures are self-reported and the time-lagged design does not fully establish causality.

## Relevance
- Risk: indirect - shows outcomes depend on collaboration quality
- Erosion: counterevidence - AI use can coincide with more meaning and engagement
- Preservation: yes - supports autonomy, competence, meaningfulness, and high-quality collaboration

## Quality
