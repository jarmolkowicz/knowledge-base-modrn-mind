# Articulate your writing preferences through examples

## Use When

AI edits keep sounding generic and you have trouble explaining what you want.

Author’s intended benefit: Make writing preferences explicit enough to use and revise.

## Try It

1. Ask AI to interview you one question at a time about sentence choices, structure, examples you like, and things you reject.
2. Answer with actual examples, rather than labels such as conversational or clear.
3. When answers conflict, inspect the examples and decide whether the preference depends on context.
4. Have AI compile the questions and your answers, separating strong boundaries from flexible preferences. [Inference] Correct the record yourself and retain unknowns rather than forcing certainty.
5. Use and revise the profile as your preferences evolve; it remains a guide to stated preferences, not a substitute for your judgment.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** First-person process account; article disclosed as AI-generated.

**Observed or reported:** Reports making a preference file after an interview and finding its output closer to his style; no independent voice or thinking test.

**Related research:** Doshi and Hauser’s short-story experiment separates ratings of individual outputs from diversity across outputs. It does not evaluate this practice or establish lasting creative skill. See [[doshi-hauser-creativity-diversity-2024]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- A text profile is not a person, identity or complete thinking process.
- The demand for exactly 100 questions and pressure not to accept uncertainty are not validated requirements.
- Removing hedging as a style rule must not erase evidential uncertainty.
