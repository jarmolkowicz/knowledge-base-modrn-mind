# Cregg & Cheavens (2023) — Healing Through Helping

## Citation

Cregg, D. R., & Cheavens, J. S. (2023). Healing through helping: an experimental investigation of kindness, social activities, and reappraisal as well-being interventions. *The Journal of Positive Psychology, 18*(6), 924–941. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2022.2154695. Published online December 12, 2022; 2023 is the journal-issue citation year.

## Type

Paper — randomized trial of three self-directed activities, with five weeks of practice and follow-up five weeks later. No AI condition.

## Key Insight

Acts of kindness showed some advantages for perceived social connection over planned social activities and thought records. The advantage depended on comparator and time point. This is evidence for an activity involving other people, not evidence that it treats AI dependence or outperforms full therapist-led CBT.

## Evidence

The trial randomized 122 adults meeting at least a mild symptom threshold for depression, anxiety, or stress to acts of kindness (40), social activities (41), or cognitive reappraisal through thought records (41). The sample included community participants and undergraduates, with mean age 24.7; symptom screening was not a clinical diagnosis (pp. 927–930).

Kindness participants performed three acts on each of two days per week for five weeks. Social-activity participants planned enjoyable activities with other people on two days per week; thought-record participants completed records at least twice weekly. All received initial instructions, a two-week phone check-in, weekly self-report measures, and a week-10 follow-up. Intent-to-treat multilevel models included baseline social support and pandemic restrictions as covariates (pp. 930–933).

For social connection, kindness exceeded social activities at week 5 (d = 0.32), but the week-10 comparison did not reach p < .05 (d = 0.41, p = .08). Versus thought records, the week-5 contrast was nonsignificant (d = 0.26, p = .12), while the week-10 contrast favored kindness (d = 0.26). Symptom-composite reductions favored kindness over thought records at both endpoints; life-satisfaction gains favored kindness at week 5, but not conventionally at week 10 (p = .07). These distinctions are explicit in the results (p. 935; Tables 3–4).

Reduced public self-absorption—preoccupation with how others see oneself—was a candidate mechanism. Time-lagged associations support it for some outcomes, but mediation paths were tested separately, and the life-satisfaction path became nonsignificant after multiple imputation (pp. 933, 935, 938). This does not establish a single causal mechanism.

## Open Questions

- Missing data differed by group, with greater loss in thought records; imputation cannot guarantee removal of attrition bias.
- Pandemic restrictions affected 24 participants and ended recruitment. Generalization beyond this sample needs testing.
- Without a no-activity group, within-group improvement does not establish absolute intervention efficacy.
- The comparison is with largely self-directed components, not full clinical care. Benefits for recipients, virtual delivery, and AI-companion users were not measured.
- This summary covers the main paper's methods, results and tables; separate supplements are not included.
