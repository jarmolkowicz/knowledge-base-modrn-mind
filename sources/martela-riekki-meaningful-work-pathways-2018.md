# Autonomy, competence, relatedness, and beneficence: A multicultural comparison of the four pathways to meaningful work

## Citation

Martela, F., & Riekki, T. J. J. (2018). Autonomy, competence, relatedness, and beneficence: A multicultural comparison of the four pathways to meaningful work. Frontiers in Psychology, 9, 1157. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01157

Supplied 14-page published PDF; PDF page locators.

## Type

Cross-sectional employee surveys in Finland and online working-participant samples in India and the USA.

## Key Insight

Felt autonomy, connection and positive contribution were associated with meaningful work across the sampled settings. Felt competence did not have a unique association in every sample; these are not four proven causal levers.

## Key Findings

- The Finnish sample combined three recruitment groups (reported N=594). Study 2 reports final samples of 342 in India and 373 in the USA, but its recruitment/exclusion arithmetic is internally inconsistent and needs checking. (PDF pp.4, 6)
- All four satisfactions had unique associations in the principal Finland and India regressions. Competence was nonsignificant in the US joint model despite a positive zero-order correlation. The Finnish post-hoc SEM also left relatedness only marginally significant. (PDF pp.5–7)
- Beneficence means perceived positive impact on others. It is examined alongside three established basic needs, not demonstrated as an established fourth basic need. (PDF pp.3–4, 10)
- Joint mediation models statistically accounted for the occupational-position association, but cross-sectional data cannot establish the proposed causal sequence. Different meaningful-work measures across studies also limit direct comparison. (PDF pp.7–11)

## Key Passages

> "our results are based on cross-sectional data and are thus correlative"
> — Martela and Riekki (2018), PDF p.10

> "the studies were based on self-reports"
> — Martela and Riekki (2018), PDF p.11

## Relevance

Connects the needs foundation with work meaning while preserving distinctions between perceived competence, actual skill and perceived contribution.

## Supports

- [[meaningful-work]] — work-specific meaning; interpretation remains bounded by this source's design and limitations.

- [[agency]] — autonomy relates to meaningfulness, without equating them.
- [[professional-identity-threat]] — contribution and connection add context, not a measured identity-loss pathway.

## Contradicts / Extends

A source of evidence for the batch's shared meaningful-work and basic-needs drafts. No separate four-step method: comparison with Lips-Wiersma is conceptual overlap, not proof that the measures are equivalent.

## Limitations

Self-report, cross-sectional associations, convenience samples, different meaning scales, inconsistencies in Study 2 exclusion counts and apparent p-sign typography. No effect sizes or total variance headline promoted. No AI exposure or objective skill outcomes.

## Open Questions

- Do interventions that change these experiences cause changes in work meaning, and for whom?
