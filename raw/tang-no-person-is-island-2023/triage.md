## Summary
Tang and colleagues study what happens when employees interact with AI rather than coworkers. Across four studies with 794 people in Taiwan, Indonesia, the United States, and Malaysia, using surveys, a field experiment, and simulation, greater AI interaction increases both need for affiliation and loneliness.

The need for affiliation can prompt more helping toward coworkers, while loneliness predicts worse after-work outcomes including insomnia and alcohol use. Effects are stronger for employees with higher attachment anxiety.
