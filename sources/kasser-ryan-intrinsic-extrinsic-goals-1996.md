# Further Examining the American Dream: Differential Correlates of Intrinsic and Extrinsic Goals

## Citation

Kasser, T., & Ryan, R. M. (1996). Further examining the American dream: Differential correlates of intrinsic and extrinsic goals. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 22(3), 280–287.

Supplied published PDF, 8 pages. Locators below refer to PDF pages.

## Type

Two correlational studies: 100 adults and 192 university students; the second included a smaller daily-report sample.

## Key Insight

What people prioritize and why they pursue it are different questions. Relative priority given to intrinsic rather than extrinsic aspirations was associated with better well-being; this does not establish that a particular goal causes well-being.

## Key Findings

- The studies compared relative centrality of intrinsic aspirations (including self-acceptance, affiliation and community) and extrinsic aspirations (money, image and fame), controlling overall ratings. They did not test a simple rule that money or recognition is harmful. (PDF pp.2–6)
- Goal ratings included importance and expected likelihood. Expected likelihood is not observed attainment. Study 2 also related aspirations to aggregated daily experiences; goals were not experimentally assigned. (PDF pp.2–6)
- The authors distinguish goal content from autonomous versus controlled reasons for pursuing a goal. Some goals do not fit a simple intrinsic/extrinsic classification. (PDF p.7)

## Key Passages

> "this is not to say that all goals are necessarily classifiable as intrinsic or extrinsic."
> — Kasser and Ryan (1996), PDF p.7

> "all data are correlational and cross-sectional, so no conclusions regarding causality can be made."
> — Kasser and Ryan (1996), PDF p.7

## Relevance

A bounded addition to [[agency]]: ownership of a goal is not the same construct as its content. AI-specific implications remain untested.

## Supports

- [[agency]] — distinguishes what a person values from whether they endorse the pursuit.

## Contradicts / Extends

Extends agency vocabulary without treating intrinsic aspirations as an agency measure or equating extrinsic goals with externally controlled motivation.

## Limitations

Correlational, largely self-report evidence from one US setting and historical period. Student and adult convenience samples; some guiding-principle measures had weak reliability. Neither causality nor generality across cultures or AI-mediated work is established.

## Open Questions

- When AI helps choose goals, does it change their content, the person's reasons for pursuing them, or both? [Inference]
