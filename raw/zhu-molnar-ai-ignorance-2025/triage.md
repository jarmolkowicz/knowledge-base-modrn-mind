## Summary
Zhu and Molnar run a preregistered experiment with 647 participants across personal and professional communication scenarios. When AI authorship is disclosed, recipients judge senders much more negatively than when the same message is labeled human-written.

In the more realistic condition where AI use is not mentioned or remains uncertain, recipients show little skepticism and form overly positive impressions. People therefore apply an AI disclosure penalty when told, but do not spontaneously discount polished messages despite widespread personal use of AI writing tools.
