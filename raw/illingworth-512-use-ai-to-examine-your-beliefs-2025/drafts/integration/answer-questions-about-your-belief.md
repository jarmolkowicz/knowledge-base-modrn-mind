# Answer questions about a belief you hold

## Use When

You want to examine a claim you keep defending, without asking AI to decide what you should believe.

Author’s intended benefit: Examine assumptions and the reasons for holding a belief.

## Try It

1. State the belief in your own words. Keep identifying or sensitive material out.
2. Ask AI for clarifying questions about assumptions and trade-offs; answer them yourself.
3. Inspect a question before answering. [Inference] Reject a premise that does not fit your experience instead of explaining why it must be true.
4. Write a sentence capturing the tension you still hold. [Inference] Identify factual claims requiring evidence outside the conversation before changing or publishing your view.

### Distinct variants

- Ramonov (387): supply relevant business context, then request three to five overlooked questions, counterarguments or risks about an idea. The user still needs to answer and verify them. [Inference] Do not equate a harsh critique with accuracy.
- Gorb (515): give three to five current ideas and background points; ask one question at a time about what starting would require you to give up, with no advice or conclusions. Define vague success expectations concretely. [Inference] Reject questions that presume hidden motives or equate discomfort with the right choice.
- Hassid (270): ask for opposing simulated perspectives after checking an unsupported business-plan assumption. [Inference] Treat the perspectives as generated possibilities; check any evidence they cite.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** First-person repeated-use account and proposed prompt.

**Observed or reported:** O’Driscoll describes questions that paused her thinking and says some positions changed; no independent assessment.

**Related research:** Guingrich, Mehta and Bhatt offer a conceptual account of [[belief-offloading]], not evidence that this routine prevents it. See [[guingrich-belief-offloading-2026]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Questions can embed a false premise about upbringing, motives or emotion.
- Felt insight is not evidence that a revised belief is true.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Keep the initial and final belief sentences and the reason for any change. A shift in certainty alone is not improvement.
