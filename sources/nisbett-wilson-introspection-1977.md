# Nisbett & Wilson (1977)

## Citation

Nisbett, R. E., & Wilson, T. D. (1977). Telling more than we can know: Verbal reports on mental processes. Psychological Review, 84(3), 231–259.

Original journal article. Locators below use PDF pages; printed pages are 230 higher.

[Retained source text](../raw/nisbett-wilson-introspection-1977/source.md).

## Type

Theoretical review of experimental evidence, including demonstrations reported by the authors; not a systematic review or meta-analysis.

## Key Insight

A convincing account of why we made a judgment is not necessarily a record of the process that caused it. This does not make every self-report false.

## Key Findings

- People sometimes failed to identify experimentally influential factors, and sometimes attributed influence to factors with no detectable effect (PDF pp.12–17).
- The authors propose that explanations often draw on plausible causal theories; observers given a description could offer similar explanations (pp.17–21).
- Reports can be accurate when relevant causes are available and plausible. Accuracy alone does not establish direct access to the underlying process (pp.21–24).
- Awareness of thoughts, feelings, intentions and intermediate results is distinct from access to the processes producing them (pp.25–26).

## Key Passages

> "We do not wish to imply that all or even most a priori causal theories are wrong."
> — Nisbett & Wilson (1977), PDF p.18

> "Confusion Between Content and Process"
> — Nisbett & Wilson (1977), PDF p.25; section heading

## Relevance

Qualifies what reflection can establish in [[metacognition]]. AI applications are inference, not findings of this pre-AI paper.

## Supports

- [[metacognition]] — distinguish reports of experience from explanations of causes.

## Contradicts / Extends

Extends reflection-based accounts by requiring external checks of causal explanations; does not refute monitoring or the value of subjective experience.

## Limitations

- Selective historical review; includes unpublished studies and anecdotes, not a modern systematic synthesis.
- Authors acknowledge demand effects, self-presentation, necessary-versus-sufficient causes, and other interpretive problems (p.16).
- Does not establish that people can never report accurately, or that all forms of introspection are invalid.
- No AI intervention, calibration training, or lasting capability outcome was tested.

## Open Questions

- When do contemporaneous records and outcome feedback improve explanations of AI-assisted decisions?
- Which forms of reflection improve monitoring without creating unsupported causal stories?
