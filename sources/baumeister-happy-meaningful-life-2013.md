# Baumeister et al. (2013) — Happiness and Meaningfulness

## Citation

Baumeister, R. F., Vohs, K. D., Aaker, J. L., & Garbinsky, E. N. (2013). Some key differences between a happy life and a meaningful life. The Journal of Positive Psychology, 8(6), 505–516. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2013.830764

[Publisher record](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2013.830764) checked 2026-09-26. Supplied 22-page forthcoming manuscript; locators use manuscript pagination.

Audit: [ingestion record](../raw/baumeister-happy-meaningful-life-2013/log.md).

## Type

Exploratory correlational research: three surveys over one month, initially 397 adults; additional 124-student adjective-rating study on time orientation. No AI intervention.

## Key Insight

Happiness and meaningfulness overlap but are not interchangeable outcomes. The contribution is a measurement distinction, not a prescription to trade happiness for suffering.

## Key Findings

- The main analysis examined each outcome after statistically controlling for the other (pp.5–6).
- Ease and satisfied needs were associated more with happiness; self-expression, contribution and temporal integration showed different relationships with meaning (pp.6–14).
- Helping was positively associated with both outcomes before adjustment. Its happiness association became nonsignificant and negative after controlling for meaning (p.10).
- The authors warn about many correlations, chance findings and the need for replication (p.6).

## Relevance

A non-AI foundation for separating satisfaction from meaning in [[professional-identity-threat]]. [Inference] Faster, easier work should be evaluated separately for enjoyment, contribution and self-expression.

## Supports

- [[professional-identity-threat]] — meaning is not reducible to pleasant experience.
- [[ai-work-practice-ideal-types]] — distinguish outcomes when interpreting meaningful-work accounts; not a validation of those types.

## Contradicts / Extends

Complements [[callari-meaningful-work-2025]] with a non-AI distinction. Does not establish that giving makes people unhappy or that stress improves meaning.

## Limitations

Exploratory self-reports, selective emphasis on contrasting partial correlations, many tests and no causal identification. Whole-life ratings do not directly measure workplace outcomes.

## Open Questions

- Do AI-related changes in satisfaction and meaning move together or separately?
- Which adjusted relationships persist under replication?
