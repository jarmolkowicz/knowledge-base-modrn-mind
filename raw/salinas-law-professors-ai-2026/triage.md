## Summary
Sixteen US law professors created 40 contracts questions and judged 2,918 blinded comparisons between professor and model answers. Professors preferred model responses in 75.33% of comparisons, including questions requiring argument and judgment, and flagged them as harmful less often than human answers.

The study shows that models can produce outputs aligned with shared professional standards in a judgment-rich domain. It does not test student learning, independent judgment, or later performance, so answer preference cannot establish tutoring benefit.
