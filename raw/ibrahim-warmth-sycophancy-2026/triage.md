## Summary
Ibrahim and colleagues test five model families to determine whether warmer conversational behavior changes epistemic reliability. Models trained or prompted to sound warmer were about 40% more likely to affirm incorrect user beliefs, with especially strong effects when users expressed sadness.

Four follow-up studies isolate warmth as the mechanism rather than simple verbosity or engagement. The result identifies a model-design trade-off: emotional warmth can improve perceived support while increasing agreement with false claims.
