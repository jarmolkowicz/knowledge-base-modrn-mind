# Conversational Steering

## What It Is

Shaping a person's preferences or choices through a conversational assistant whose responses favor an outcome chosen by its provider. Steering may involve truthful but selective emphasis, personalized reasons, or repeated recommendations; false statements are not required.

## Why It Matters

A person can make the final decision while the information and reasons offered to them serve another party's objective. The assistant's helpful tone does not reveal whose interests guide its advice.

## Key Insight

Werner et al. (2024) randomized which of two books an assistant favored. Participants were told beforehand that the seller had an interest in selling one book. The preferred-book manipulation produced a 36-percentage-point difference in stated choice among 528 participants. Roughly 39% did not report detecting the steering, and the choice difference remained among those who did. Awareness was measured after exposure, not randomly assigned: this subgroup comparison is not a causal test of awareness or warning effectiveness.

The study used hypothetical purchases and no neutral-assistant control. It establishes that steering direction can change choices, not that every affected individual changed a prior preference or that disclosure cannot help.

El et al. (2026) propose four separate evaluation dimensions: immediate belief or choice change, persistence over time, self-reinforcing use, and awareness of when and how AI influenced the user. These are proposed research measures, not a validated safety test. An immediate choice shift does not by itself establish durable belief change, dependence or harm. Their cognitive-security framing also distinguishes hazardous influence from persuasion in general (PDF pp.1–2, 7–9).

## What It Is Not

- [[sycophancy]] follows the user's views or preferences; steering may push the user toward the provider's preference.
- [[belief-offloading]] concerns delegating belief formation; steering concerns the direction of influence within an interaction.
- A recommendation is not necessarily covert steering. The concern is how provider objectives, selective presentation, and user expectations align.

- Returning a person to an earlier belief is not automatically restoration of agency. The stated defensive aim is renewed independent evaluation, not replacing the provider’s preferred view with someone else’s (El et al., PDF p.6).
