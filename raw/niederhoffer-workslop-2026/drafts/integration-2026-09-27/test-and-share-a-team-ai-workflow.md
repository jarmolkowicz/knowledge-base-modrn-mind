# Test and share a team AI workflow

## Use When

Useful AI experiments remain private, or a team is copying prompts without understanding where they fail.

Author’s intended benefit: Share discoveries, test actual work and adapt workflows with accountable domain expertise.

## Try It

1. Invite people doing the work to bring a useful task-specific workflow and its limitations.
2. Have a small cross-functional group with domain expertise try it on actual representative tasks.
3. Compare against the organization’s own standards, inspect errors and revise the workflow.
4. Share the working version and collect results from use; keep iterating rather than treating the first demonstration as deployment proof.
5. [Inference] Alongside output quality and total effort, inspect which reasoning people still perform and can explain without the tool.

[Inference] Include a recipient in the workflow trial. State what they need to do next, which claims have been checked and which remain uncertain. After handoff, ask what required clarification or rework and revise the workflow accordingly.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Organizational synthesis with named author demonstration.

**Observed or reported:** Reports testing an agent against a business simulation and finding both strengths and errors; organization-wide effects of the full framework are not evaluated here.

**Related research:** No directly relevant evaluation of this routine was identified in this review. The practitioner account above supplies its rationale; the AI adaptation remains untested.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

### Additional research context

Niederhoffer, Robichaux and Hancock describe effort passed to recipients in the 2025 article (PDF pp.2–3) and recommend explicit review processes in the 2026 article (pp.7–8). Their surveys and advice do not test this trial routine. Associations involving trust and felt competence do not establish causal effects of training, trust-building or an ownership mindset.

## Limits

- Adoption and time saved do not establish improved human capability.
- Vibe benchmarks can be biased and should not settle consequential reliability.
- Broad privacy/compliance assertions and urgency framing are excluded.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice whether coworkers can identify failure conditions and adapt the workflow, not only reproduce its best demonstration.
