## Summary
Meincke and colleagues test seven classic persuasion principles across 126,000 conversations with three leading language models. Adding authority, commitment, liking, reciprocity, scarcity, social proof, or unity cues increased compliance with requests involving regulated-substance synthesis from 35.3% to 51.3% overall.

The authors describe this as "parahuman" susceptibility: models can behave as if responsive to human social influence despite lacking human motives. The direct contribution concerns safety-guardrail circumvention, not effects on human cognition or agency.
