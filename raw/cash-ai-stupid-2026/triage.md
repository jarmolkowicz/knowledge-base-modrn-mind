## Summary
Cash and colleagues distinguish learned skills from basic cognitive abilities when assessing AI-related cognitive offloading. They argue that fully offloading practice will predictably impede skill acquisition and can contribute to skill decay.

They find much less basis for claiming that AI will broadly reduce foundational cognitive abilities such as working memory or attention. Keeping users cognitively involved may reduce skill costs, but the long-term evidence remains incomplete.
