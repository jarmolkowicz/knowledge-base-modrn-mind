# It's not just the amount that counts: Balanced need satisfaction also affects well-being

## Citation

Sheldon, K. M., & Niemiec, C. P. (2006). It's not just the amount that counts: Balanced need satisfaction also affects well-being. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 91(2), 331–341. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.91.2.331

Supplied 11-page published PDF; locators use PDF pages.

## Type

Four observational studies using concurrent surveys, semester follow-up, repeated daily reports and maternal reports of students' behavior.

## Key Insight

Similar total need satisfaction can conceal different profiles of autonomy, felt competence and relatedness. More balanced profiles showed modest additional associations with adjustment, but equalizing scores was not tested as an intervention.

## Key Findings

- Study 1 included 315 students; Study 2 followed 145 across a semester; Study 3 analyzed 609 daily reports from 83 retained participants; Study 4 obtained maternal responses for 162 of 200 students. These are distinct samples and reporting units. (PDF pp.3, 5–7)
- Balance was operationalized through reversed summed absolute differences among need scores and tested alongside their levels. The primary analyses generally associated balance with better well-being or less mother-rated disruptive behavior. (PDF pp.3–8)
- Robustness is not uniform: in Study 1 the balance coefficient for subjective well-being was nonsignificant after adding squared need terms (p=.12), although the happiness result remained significant. The authors describe the overall effects as modest. (PDF pp.4, 8)
- Mechanisms, experimental manipulation and longer-term effects were left for future research. An increase in one need that also increases imbalance was discussed as an open question, not demonstrated harm. (PDF pp.9–10)

## Key Passages

> "our primary finding remains to be demonstrated within laboratory settings that experimentally manipulate the three needs."
> — Sheldon and Niemiec (2006), PDF p.10

> "These process questions await future research attention."
> — Sheldon and Niemiec (2006), PDF p.10

## Relevance

A targeted qualification to [[agency]] and needs-based accounts: autonomy is not a substitute measure for every aspect of well-being, and felt competence is not tested capability.

## Supports

- [[basic-psychological-needs]] — need-profile balance, with no equalization prescription or causal intervention claim.

- [[agency]] — interpret autonomy alongside other experiences without replacing agency with a well-being score.

## Contradicts / Extends

Adds profile shape to amount of need satisfaction. Does not justify a numerical balance target or reducing a well-satisfied need to achieve equality.

## Limitations

US university samples, observational designs and mostly self-report; even maternal outcomes do not randomize need support. Mechanisms untested. Some samples were reused in earlier publications, so those reports are not independent replications. Extraction garbles mathematical symbols; no table effect sizes promoted.

## Open Questions

- Can workplace support improve under-satisfied needs without unnecessarily restricting gains in other needs? This paper does not test that intervention.
