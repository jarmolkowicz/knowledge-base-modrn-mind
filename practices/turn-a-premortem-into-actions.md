# Turn a premortem into actions and stopping criteria

## Use When

An important launch has risks people hesitate to raise.

Author’s intended benefit: Expose threats early and convert concerns into mitigation or stopping actions.

## Try It

1. Bring relevant functions together and imagine the project has failed at a future date.
2. Give participants quiet time to record real threats, apparent threats they consider less serious, and unspoken concerns. Keep entries hidden initially.
3. Share concerns. Ask each participant to identify a serious threat raised by someone else.
4. The leader prioritizes and publishes an action plan, then remains accountable for follow-through.
5. Annie Duke’s variant (550): specify observable failure signals in advance and pair each with an action, further inquiry, pivot or stop.
6. [Inference] If AI adds scenarios, do so after independent human input and keep generated possibilities separate from observed warning signals.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Repeated professional facilitation account and detailed procedure.

**Observed or reported:** Reports surfacing overlooked risks and teams later reusing a shared vocabulary; avoided failures remain counterfactual.

**Related research:** The practitioner account provides the rationale, not a direct evaluation; the AI adaptation remains untested.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Benefits are practitioner reports, not a controlled comparison.
- A vote for a frightening threat does not estimate its probability.
- An action plan and follow-through matter; a list of risks alone is insufficient.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice whether a concern changed an action or stopping rule. A longer risk list is not a safer project.
