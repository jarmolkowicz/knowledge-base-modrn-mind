### Hai: replace both paragraphs beginning "Hai et al. (2025) provide" and "The empirical leg"

Hai et al. (2025) studied daily GenAI collaboration, work alienation and employee expediency in an experience-sampling field study (229 employees; 1,050 within-person observations). Higher daily collaboration intensity was associated with greater same-day alienation and an indirect association with expediency. The conditional indirect association was significant under high digital job demands, not under low demands. This observational design does not establish that GenAI caused alienation, that employees engaged passively, or that alienation is the same construct as perceived autonomy. The authors recommend keeping employees in control of consequential work; that recommendation was not experimentally tested. See [[hai-dark-side-collaboration-2025]].

### Wu: replace both paragraphs beginning "Wu et al. (2025) supply" and "The implication for the entry's"

Wu et al. (2025) tested short text-generation and brainstorming tasks in four preregistered experiments (total N=3,562). In Study 4, moving from solo work to ChatGPT collaboration was accompanied by a drop in perceived control (change −1.01; d=0.84), while moving from collaboration to solo work raised control (change +0.42; reported d=−0.39). Control remained stable across two collaborative tasks. These results concern the tested task sequences and perceived control, not evidence that every AI workflow suppresses agency or that sustained use cannot support it. They do not measure long-term retained capability. See [[wu-collaboration-motivation-2025]].

[Inference] Compare autonomy results against the actual alternative: working alone is not the same comparator as working with an unfamiliar human. Wu's short experimental transitions and Hai's daily field associations concern different outcomes and designs; they do not demonstrate one shared causal mechanism. Corgnet et al.'s human-teammate comparison below is a boundary on broad claims about AI presence, not a direct replication or refutation of Wu. Practical proposals to increase user input and meaningful control require testing in their intended setting.

## Proposed Additions

### To "Key Insight" section

Corgnet, Fumagalli and Rezaei (2026) qualify claims that AI presence necessarily lowers perceived control. In a preregistered online experiment (N = 1,091), participants paired with ChatGPT-5.2 reported higher autonomy than those paired with an unfamiliar human for a short creative task (d = 0.480, Table B.5). This comparison differs from AI versus solo work. It measures perceived autonomy, not retained decision skill or authentic values, and provides no long-term evidence. Willingness to repeat the task alone did not meaningfully differ between groups.
