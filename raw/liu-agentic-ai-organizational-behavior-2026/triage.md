## Summary
Liu argues that collectives of AI agents are partial organizational analogues. They divide work, coordinate dependencies, run routines, and create collective outputs, but these behaviors depend on prompts, memory, schemas, tools, permissions, and traces rather than motivation, identity, trust, or moral responsibility.

Simulations and agent traces suggest that copying human organizational forms can add lossy handoffs, correlated errors, and verification work. Shared state and adaptive coordination perform better when context stays durable and inspectable. The paper calls the resulting burden "contextual transaction cost."
