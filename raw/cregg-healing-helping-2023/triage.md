## Summary
Cregg and Cheavens randomly assigned 122 people with elevated anxiety or depression symptoms to acts of kindness, social activities, or cognitive reappraisal. Acts of kindness produced greater gains in social connection than both comparison conditions and greater gains in symptoms and life satisfaction than reappraisal.

Reduced public self-absorption partly explained the benefit. The study offers a concrete human-to-human practice that may protect connection when AI companionship or frictionless support displaces reciprocal social action.
