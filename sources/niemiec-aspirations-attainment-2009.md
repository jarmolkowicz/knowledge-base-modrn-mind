# The path taken: Consequences of attaining intrinsic and extrinsic aspirations in post-college life

## Citation

Niemiec, C. P., Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2009). The path taken: Consequences of attaining intrinsic and extrinsic aspirations in post-college life. Journal of Research in Personality. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrp.2008.09.001

Supplied 32-page NIH author manuscript. Volume and pagination are uncertain in this manuscript. DOI and year appear on the manuscript.

## Type

Two-wave observational survey, one year apart, of recent US college graduates: 164 responses at the first post-college wave, 147 at the second.

## Key Insight

Goal attainment should not be treated as a uniform well-being benefit. Associations varied with goal content and with whether results were unadjusted or estimated in a joint model.

## Key Findings

- Participants self-reported importance, current attainment, need satisfaction and psychological health. Current attainment is neither expected future success nor independently verified achievement. (PDF pp.6–7)
- In joint models, intrinsic aspiration attainment was associated with better well-being and less ill-being. Extrinsic attainment was unrelated to well-being and positively associated with ill-being in the wave-two model; its association with change in ill-being was only marginal (p<.07). (PDF pp.9–14)
- The unadjusted correlations did not show the same adverse extrinsic-attainment pattern: the authors report modest positive well-being correlations and little relation to ill-being before modeling competing variance. (PDF pp.15–16)
- Changes in need satisfaction statistically mediated intrinsic-attainment associations. With two observational waves, the authors explicitly reject conclusions about causal direction. (PDF pp.10–13, 17)

## Key Passages

> "all data were collected with self-report measures."
> — Niemiec et al. (2009), PDF p.17

> "our findings were correlational, so they do not provide evidence for the direction of effects among the constructs under investigation."
> — Niemiec et al. (2009), PDF p.17

## Relevance

Qualifies success-based accounts of [[agency]]: achieving a goal, endorsing it, feeling effective and being well are separate judgments.

## Supports

- [[agency]] — goal content and psychological outcomes cannot be read from achievement alone.

## Contradicts / Extends

Moves beyond expected likelihood and relative priority in Kasser and Ryan (1996) to reported attainment and change. Does not show that wealth or success generally damages well-being.

## Limitations

Homogeneous recent-graduate sample, self-report, attrition and two-wave correlational mediation. Adjusted and unadjusted results differ. Some project data appeared in earlier research; not automatically an independent replication. No AI task or objective capability test.

## Open Questions

- Does AI-supported goal attainment improve what the person values, or merely improve achievement on a selected target? [Inference]
