# How do human resource managers make sense of their work?

## Citation

Szőts-Kováts, K., & Primecz, H. (2025). How do human resource managers make sense of their work? A study of changing work meaningfulness using the critical incident technique. Human Resource Development International, 28(1), 132–151. https://doi.org/10.1080/13678868.2024.2310656

Published online in 2024; supplied issue citation is 2025. Twenty-one-page PDF including cover; locators use PDF pages.

## Type

Interpretive retrospective study of 37 critical incidents narrated by 16 HR managers from different organizations; interviews conducted and analyzed in Hungarian.

## Key Insight

A difficult or positive incident can change how a person interprets the value of their work, but an incident need not change its meaning. Reported enduring reinterpretation is not the same as prospectively measured lasting change.

## Key Findings

- The authors distinguish scenarios of initial loss of meaning followed by reinterpretation and action, unstable change with unresolved tension, positive reinforcement, and no change. These categories describe narratives, not a universal sequence. (PDF pp.9–15)
- The analysis connects more enduring reported changes with reconsideration of work identity, values and sources of significance. Four incidents were explicitly classified as no change. (PDF pp.12–15)
- Member checking covered transcripts and initial coding; the authors retained counterexamples. The temporal account nevertheless comes from retrospective interviews, not repeated real-time measurement. (PDF pp.7–9, 17)

## Key Passages

> "the retrospective approach to critical incidents is necessarily less detailed and precise than any real time data collection or diary method."
> — Szőts-Kováts and Primecz (2025), PDF p.17

> "There were four cases in which no change could be identified"
> — Szőts-Kováts and Primecz (2025), PDF p.14

## Relevance

Targeted background for [[professional-identity-threat]]: changes in work may prompt reinterpretation, stability or unresolved tension. No AI-specific identity mechanism was tested.

## Supports

- [[professional-identity-threat]] — distinguishes work events from workers' interpretations and possible changes in identity or values.

## Contradicts / Extends

Connects episodic experiences with more enduring views of work while preserving negative cases. Does not prove that reflection, coaching or AI adoption causes positive transformation.

## Limitations

Sixteen purposively selected managers, one occupational and language context, retrospective narratives and interpretive categorization. Thirty-seven incidents are not 37 independent participants. Two interviewees were no longer HR managers at interview. Persistence and causal sequence were not prospectively verified.

## Open Questions

- Which AI-related events leave meaning unchanged, and which prompt reassessment of contribution or values? [Inference]
