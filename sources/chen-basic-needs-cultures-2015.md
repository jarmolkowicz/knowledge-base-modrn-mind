# Basic psychological need satisfaction, need frustration, and need strength across four cultures

## Citation

Chen, B., Vansteenkiste, M., Beyers, W., Boone, L., Deci, E. L., Van der Kaap-Deeder, J., Duriez, B., Lens, W., Matos, L., Mouratidis, A., Ryan, R. M., Sheldon, K. M., Soenens, B., Van Petegem, S., & Verstuyf, J. (2015). Basic psychological need satisfaction, need frustration, and need strength across four cultures. Motivation and Emotion, 39, 216–236. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-014-9450-1

Supplied 21-page revised proof dated 2014; published citation year 2015 corroborated by the Martela et al. (2021) reference list. PDF locators retain proof pagination.

## Type

Two cross-sectional student studies; Study 2 developed and cross-validated a 24-item satisfaction/frustration measure across Belgium, China, the USA and Peru.

## Key Insight

Need satisfaction, active frustration and wanting or valuing a need are different constructs. Their relationships with well-being cannot be represented by one simple good-to-bad score.

## Key Findings

- Study 2 included 1,051 students, split into scale-development (525) and cross-validation (526) samples. A six-factor structure distinguished satisfaction and frustration for each of autonomy, competence and relatedness; metric equivalence was examined after item selection. (PDF pp.8–12)
- Need satisfaction had stronger associations with vitality and life satisfaction; frustration had stronger associations with depressive symptoms. Individual needs did not uniquely predict every outcome: competence satisfaction was nonsignificant for life satisfaction in Study 2, and relatedness had no unique Study 1 well-being association. (PDF pp.7–8, 13–16)
- Moderation by reported need valuation or desire was not detected. This supports the theory within these samples; nonsignificance does not prove identical effects for every person or culture. (PDF pp.15–18)
- Study 1 reporting requires caution: the abstract says 685 participants, while methods list 324 Chinese and 359 Belgian participants (sum 683). Chinese autonomy satisfaction and valuation reliabilities were low. (PDF pp.1, 5–8)

## Key Passages

> "the data were cross-sectional in nature, preventing us from drawing any causal conclusions."
> — Chen et al. (2015), PDF p.18

> "autonomy refers to the experience of self-determination, full willingness, and volition when carrying out an activity."
> — Chen et al. (2015), PDF p.2

## Relevance

Foundational definitions for [[agency]], [[ai-self-efficacy-erosion]] and [[professional-identity-threat]]. Defines [[basic-psychological-needs]] without conflating needs with identity or skill.

## Supports

- [[basic-psychological-needs]] — measured construct; separates satisfaction, frustration and need strength.
- [[agency]] — volition is distinct from independence and from the KB's broader agency construct.

## Contradicts / Extends

Provides cross-country scale evidence and a satisfaction/frustration distinction beyond the existing AI-focused theoretical summaries. Beneficence is not established as a fourth basic need by this paper.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional convenience samples, self-report and country as a proxy rather than directly measured culture. Study 1 includes Chinese high-school students despite the broad university-student wording in the limitations. Revised proof contains a sample-total inconsistency. No objective ability test or AI exposure.
