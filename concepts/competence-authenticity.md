# Competence Authenticity

## What It Is

The perceived alignment between work results and one's own mastery, understanding, and effort. Frenzel-Piasentin, Finze, and Kosok (2026, PDF p.10) use the term for whether AI-supported performance feels grounded in personal competence.

## Why It Matters

Good results can coexist with little understanding. Equally, someone who supplied the ideas and used AI only for proofreading may feel that the work no longer counts as theirs. Output quality alone cannot distinguish these experiences.

## Key Insight

The construct concerns the perceived origin of performance. It does not measure actual skill. In five focus groups with 22 participants, the authors identified both competence gain without effort and self-authenticity concerns. [Inference] A practical discussion should therefore ask separately what the worker understands, what they contributed, and how they feel about that contribution.

Evidence remains exploratory. The study did not validate a scale, measure changes in ability, or test an intervention. It does not establish that AI assistance makes work inauthentic.

## Boundary with AI Impostor Feelings

[Inference] This is broader than the [[ai-impostor-phenomenon]]: competence authenticity concerns perceived alignment between results and personal mastery, understanding, and effort; impostor feelings concern a specific fraudulence response after success. Someone may question that alignment without feeling fraudulent, or feel fraudulent despite a substantial contribution. This is an editorial distinction between the papers' constructs, not evidence that they are empirically independent. Neither supplied study tests that independence.
