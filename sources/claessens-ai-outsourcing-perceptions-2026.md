# Negative Perceptions of Outsourcing to Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Claessens, S., Veitch, P., & Everett, J. A. C. (2026). Negative perceptions of outsourcing to artificial intelligence. Computers in Human Behavior, 177, 108894. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108894

Journal issue 2026; online publication December 2025. Supplied 20-page PDF. Abstract total N=3,935 conflicts with reported final study samples (466, 762, 766, 753, 758, 610; sum 4,115). Total unresolved; do not cite a settled combined sample.

## Type

Six experiments with UK Prolific convenience samples, reported as preregistered. Participants evaluated descriptions of task completion; Studies 3 and 6 also showed writing. Bayesian ordinal multilevel models; Study 6 added a path model of perceived effort, authenticity and care.

## Key Insight

Judgments of AI outsourcing depend on the task and how it is done. Perceived care and authenticity matter particularly for relational tasks, but these are observers' judgments—not proof of the user's actual care, competence or morality.

## Key Findings

- Study 1 compared doing a task oneself, human outsourcing and AI outsourcing. Negative judgments varied across tasks and traits; the findings are not uniquely an anti-AI effect.
- Study 2 distinguished AI assistance with human editing from copying output, and acknowledgment from non-acknowledgment. Full outsourcing was rated more negatively overall than assistance. Not acknowledging use harmed morality and trust ratings. Assistance plus honesty could still attract negative judgments on relational tasks; pooled ratings did not show a universal penalty on every trait.
- Study 3 held writing constant across authorship descriptions. AI-attributed writing was rated less meaningful and authentic and received lower grades and hypothetical rewards. Character and most work ratings did not clearly differ between assistance and full outsourcing; task-type contrasts were uncertain. The authors summarize writing quality as similar, not a formal equivalence result.
- Study 4 found benefits to described prompting effort on some judgments, but no clear effect of a personalized versus generic model. This null does not establish that authenticity is irrelevant.
- Study 5 found no clear pooled effect of stated reasons for AI use on character ratings. Some relational-task judgments improved when use was motivated by doing the task well rather than rushing, without removing all negative impressions.
- Study 6's love-letter scenario linked AI outsourcing to lower perceived effort, authenticity and care. The proposed mediation sequence is consistent with associations among measured perceptions; randomizing outsourcing does not establish causal ordering among those mediators.

## Key Passages

> "these negative perceptions were not uniform"
> — Claessens et al. (2026), PDF p.4

> "our findings reflect people’s social perceptions of AI use and should not be used to draw normative conclusions"
> — Claessens et al. (2026), PDF p.19

## Relevance

Direct AI evidence for [[authenticity]], [[disclosure-penalty]] and [[effort-heuristic]], with task-sensitive limits. No measured long-term relationships, actual skill loss or actual moral character.

## Supports

- [[authenticity]] — identical-text judgments can differ by described production process.
- [[disclosure-penalty]] — social penalties are conditional; honesty and the extent of outsourcing must be separated.
- [[effort-heuristic]] — perceived effort can inform value and character judgments, not just quality.

## Contradicts / Extends

Qualifies uniform disclosure-penalty language. Study 2's honest assistance condition had higher pooled morality and trust ratings than the unaided control, alongside greater perceived laziness. Specific relational tasks can show different patterns. Do not turn this into advice to conceal AI use.

## Limitations

UK convenience samples; task descriptions and hypothetical rewards, not observed workplace or relationship outcomes. Study 3 preregistration archive timestamp was lost; authors report a timestamped uploaded document remains. The preregistration document and supplementary data are not included in this summary. Aggregate sample conflict remains unresolved. Speculation about virtue erosion or weaker social ties is not tested.

## Open Questions

- Which patterns persist in real relationships, other cultures and repeated use?
- Does assistance for disability or low writing confidence change judgments?
- What explains the conflicting total sample count?
