# Illusion of Explanatory Depth

## Evidence Limits

Rozenblit and Keil's original experiments concern explanatory knowledge of devices and natural phenomena. The AI-specific risks below come from Messeri and Crockett's Perspective, which does not establish a universal effect of AI use.

## What It Is

The tendency to overestimate the depth of one's explanatory knowledge. In Rozenblit and Keil's experiments, participants rated their understanding lower after trying to explain mechanisms. The effect was stronger for explanatory knowledge than for the comparison knowledge domains; it is not a claim that overconfidence occurs only for explanations.

## Why It Matters

[Inference] Fluent explanations can make gaps in understanding harder to notice. Whether consulting AI changes a person's explanatory knowledge or confidence requires assessment; a convincing output alone establishes neither.

Messeri & Crockett (2024) discuss this illusion as a risk of AI use in science. Their **prediction–explanation fallacy** [p.4] concerns treating predictive success as explanatory understanding. An accurate predictive model need not capture the process that generated the data; the distinction matters when interpreting what a model has established.

The authors suggest that use outside a scientist's expertise may make evaluation harder. Their Perspective does not establish that this is always the highest-risk setting.

## Key Insight

Messeri and Crockett argue that fluency and apparent simplicity may encourage a feeling of understanding (p.4). The proposed application to AI is distinct from Rozenblit and Keil's experiments.

[Inference] Reflection exercise adapted from Rozenblit and Keil: rate understanding on a 1–7 scale, try a step-by-step explanation, then reassess. A lower rating may reveal unnoticed gaps; the change alone is not a validated individual diagnosis or an objective capability measure. The original procedure also used scale training, diagnostic questions and expert explanations (pp.526–529).

## Proposed AI-Related Risks

Messeri and Crockett propose several ways AI use could encourage illusions of understanding:

1. Fluent language may be mistaken for an adequate explanation.
2. Predictive accuracy may be mistaken for understanding a mechanism.
3. Use outside the user's expertise may make verification harder.
4. Aggregating results from many models may make limitations harder to track.

These are arguments for scrutiny, not a demonstrated causal sequence shared by all AI systems or users.
