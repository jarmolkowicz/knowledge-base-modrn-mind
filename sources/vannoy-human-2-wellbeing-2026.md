# Vannoy, Cadieux & Lyubomirsky (2026)

## Citation

Vannoy, T. K., Cadieux, S., & Lyubomirsky, S. (2026). Human 2.0? AI and the future of well-being, connection, and personal growth: A narrative review. Behavioral Sciences, 16, 909. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16060909

Published June 3, 2026; supplied publisher PDF, 21 pages.

[Retained source text](../raw/vannoy-human-2-wellbeing-2026/source.md).

## Type

Selective narrative review across psychological interventions, education, coaching, communication and companionship; no new participants or pooled effect.

## Key Insight

Separate AI delivering a structured activity, AI helping people communicate, and AI becoming the relationship itself. A result from one setting cannot automatically be transferred to another.

## Key Findings

- Reviews some short-term benefits from structured support, learning and coaching, alongside mixed or null comparisons with non-AI activities (pp.3–6).
- In several interventions, established exercises, reflection or feedback may be the active ingredient; AI's added benefit remains uncertain (pp.4–5,14).
- Communication effects depend partly on perceived authorship and context; vignette findings and real-time exchanges are not equivalent (pp.7–9).
- Companionship findings include temporary relief and concerns about dependence; longer-term effects and differences between users remain unresolved (pp.9–15).

## Key Passages

> "rather than an exhaustive systematic review."
> — Vannoy, Cadieux & Lyubomirsky (2026), PDF p.2

> "these benefits are neither universal nor clearly attributable to AI itself."
> — Vannoy, Cadieux & Lyubomirsky (2026), PDF p.14; line wrapping normalized

## Relevance

Distinguishes use contexts relevant to [[ai-loneliness-effect]]. Its discussion of existing studies is not additional independent evidence.

## Supports

- [[ai-loneliness-effect]] — separate immediate feelings, sustained loneliness and human relationships.

## Contradicts / Extends

Includes potential benefits rather than treating all social AI use as harmful. Does not establish that early relief causes subsequent dependence.

## Limitations

- Selective narrative review; no exhaustive search, pooled estimate or formal risk-of-bias synthesis.
- Groups older scripted tools and contemporary LLMs; purpose-built studies may not generalize to public chatbots.
- Broad review summaries remain subject to the primary studies' design limits. In Fang, randomized conditions and self-selected usage support different inferences; Folk and Dunn use distinct loneliness measures.
- Proposed long-term cognitive and social costs are not uniformly demonstrated outcomes, nor is the review clinical guidance.

## Open Questions

- Does AI add benefit beyond the same activity without AI?
- Do benefits persist and transfer to human relationships or unaided skills?
