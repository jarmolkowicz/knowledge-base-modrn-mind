# Runco & Jaeger (2012) — The Standard Definition of Creativity

## Citation

Runco, M. A., & Jaeger, G. J. (2012). The standard definition of creativity. *Creativity Research Journal*, 24(1), 92–96.

**DOI:** [10.1080/10400419.2012.650092](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400419.2012.650092)

## Type

Paper (definitional / historical correction)

## Key Insight

Runco and Jaeger describe creativity as requiring both originality and effectiveness—also expressed as usefulness, fit, appropriateness or value. Novelty alone is insufficient, as is usefulness without originality. They trace a clear historical formulation to Stein (1953), followed by Barron (1955), while discussing earlier precursors.

The definition specifies criteria without settling who should judge them or whether further criteria, such as surprise, are needed. The paper predates current generative AI and does not establish that AI has solved originality or that humans contribute only evaluation.

## Key Passages

> "The standard definition is bipartite: Creativity requires both originality and effectiveness."
> — Runco & Jaeger, [p.2]

> "Originality is vital for creativity but is not sufficient. Ideas and products that are merely original might very well be useless. They may be unique or uncommon for good reason! Originality can be found in the word salad of a psychotic and can be produced by monkeys on word processors. A truly random process will often generate something that is merely original."
> — Runco & Jaeger, [p.2]

> "Effectiveness may take the form of value… [Bethune 1839] described value as: 'The stability of the fabric which gives perpetuity to the decoration. To mingle the useful with the beautiful, is the highest style of art.'"
> — Runco & Jaeger, [p.2-3]

> "The standard definition only pinpoints which criteria must be used; it does not say anything about who is to judge each, and who is to judge the judges."
> — Runco & Jaeger, [p.5]

> "[Stein 1953]: 'The creative work is a novel work that is accepted as tenable or useful or satisfying by a group in some point in time.'"
> — Runco & Jaeger, [p.4] (quoting Stein 1953)

## Relevance

[Inference] AI-assisted work can be assessed separately for originality and effectiveness in its intended context. Individual output quality and diversity across a collection are different outcomes; collective diversity is not a synonym for effectiveness.

- [[strategic-alternation]] and [[think-first]] — [Inference] possible creative practices, not tested here.

## Contradicts / Extends

Discusses competing definitions, including Simonton's proposal to add surprise. The definitional framework can guide later studies, but does not establish their causal findings.

## Open Questions

- Who should judge effectiveness: author, audience, domain experts or others affected by the work?
- What counts as novel, relative to which prior work and audience?
- How should originality, usefulness and collective diversity be assessed separately in AI-assisted work?
