# Keep your sensory association in the work

## Use When

A creative tool turns a personal sensory association into a factual correction.

Author’s intended benefit: Notice whether a tool respects a stated sensory association rather than correcting it as a factual error.

## Try It

1. Choose a harmless sensory question, such as a colour associated with a day.
2. Ask briefly for a response without correction. If needed, clarify that you are exploring an association, not a factual claim.
3. Compare the generated association with your own; you need not adopt its answer.
4. Supply your own association and ask for a small scene that preserves it.
5. [Inference] Judge the scene against the experience you intended to express, rather than treating the model’s explanation as perception.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Proposed exercise and first-person exploration.

**Observed or reported:** Byron reports questioning Lumo about sensory and mathematical transformations; no accessibility evaluation.

**Related research:** Doshi and Hauser’s short-story experiment separates ratings of individual outputs from diversity across outputs. It does not evaluate this practice or establish lasting creative skill. See [[doshi-hauser-creativity-diversity-2024]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- The model has no demonstrated felt association; its explanation is generated language.
- One response cannot diagnose a neurotypical system or represent all neurodiverse users.
- Respecting a creative association differs from agreeing with a harmful factual belief.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Identify one place where the system preserved or flattened your association. Keep subjective expression distinct from factual verification.
