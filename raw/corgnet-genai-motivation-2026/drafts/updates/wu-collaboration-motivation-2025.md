# Update: Wu et al. (2025) — GenAI Collaboration and Motivation

## Source

## Proposed Additions

### To "Contradicts / Extends" section

- Qualified by Corgnet, Fumagalli and Rezaei (2026): in a preregistered creative-task experiment, human–AI participants reported greater satisfaction, interest, autonomy, competence, and relatedness than newly formed human–human pairs. The comparison was with unfamiliar collaborators, not solo work or established colleagues. Behavioral willingness to repeat the task alone did not meaningfully increase. The findings show why Wu's task-transition results should not become a claim that AI collaboration always harms motivation; the studies do not estimate the same effect.
