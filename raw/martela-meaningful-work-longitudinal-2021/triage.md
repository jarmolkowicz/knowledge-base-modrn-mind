## Summary

The paper examines whether autonomy, competence, relatedness and beneficence predict later meaningful work. Autonomy and beneficence predicted subsequent meaning after adjustment for baseline meaning and the other needs; competence and relatedness did not show the same prospective pattern.

This qualifies the broader cross-sectional four-pathway account. The authors explicitly say temporal precedence does not demonstrate causality. Attrition, self-report, a relatively advantaged one-country sample and the adapted meaning scale require attention in full review.

## Quality

- Evidence basis: Three-wave observational study of Chilean workers: T1 631, T2 240, T3 148.
- Originality: Compared with [[callari-meaningful-work-2025]], [[agency]] and the batch's Martela & Riekki (2018). Adds temporal evidence and a meaningful boundary to the four-pathway account.
- Limits: Large attrition, possible confounding, self-reports, one country and six-month span. Null adjusted predictions do not prove competence or relatedness is irrelevant.
