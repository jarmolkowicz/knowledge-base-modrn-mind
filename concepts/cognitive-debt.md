# Cognitive Debt

## What It Is

A proposed accumulated cost of relying on AI when assisted task completion replaces opportunities to develop or assess unaided skills. The debt metaphor is a hypothesis about learning over time, not a measured quantity or an inevitable effect of each AI use.

## Why It Matters

Assisted output can conceal gaps in unaided performance. The size, persistence and reversibility of those gaps depend on the task and how AI is used; a short-term difference does not establish compounding skill loss.

Bartoš et al. (2026) discuss cognitive offloading and Kosmyna's writing study as possible explanations for variation in AI-supported learning. Their bias-adjusted estimate remains positive (SMD = 0.196, 95% credible interval [0.000, 0.323]; BF₁₀ = 13.3) and is much smaller than the unadjusted estimate. Publication-bias adjustment does not test a cognitive-debt mechanism or establish a null learning effect.

Stadler, Bannert & Sailer (2024) found lower self-reported germane cognitive load and weaker same-session justifications after ChatGPT-3.5 versus Google research (N=91). Their exploratory mediation model is consistent with a role for active processing, but does not establish that load caused the outcome or directly measure schema construction. The study did not measure accumulated debt, delayed retention or lasting capability loss.

## Key Insight

The linked Kosmyna et al. (2025) source reports differences in EEG connectivity during writing, poorer essay-content recall among LLM users and more homogeneous outputs. These measures are not a direct measure of accumulated skill loss. The debt interpretation should remain distinct from the observations; Bartoš's discussion does not independently replicate them.

## The Debt Mechanism

[Speculation] A possible sequence is that repeated substitution reduces relevant practice, which limits skill development and increases future reliance. The cited studies do not establish this complete feedback loop, inevitable inability to work alone, or a debt amount that compounds with every use.

## Not All AI Use Is Equal

[Inference] Copying an answer, jointly developing it, and requesting critique after an independent attempt offer different opportunities for practice. These are task-design distinctions, not validated high-, medium- or low-debt categories. Assess unaided performance rather than assigning a debt score from usage alone.

## Professional Implication

[Inference] When maintaining a skill matters, include opportunities to practise and assess it without AI. Distinguish failure to acquire a new skill from loss of an existing one, and a temporary withdrawal effect from lasting impairment. This entry does not validate a particular practice schedule.
