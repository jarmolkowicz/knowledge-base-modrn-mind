# Ali et al. (2025)

## Citation

Ali, I., Nguyen, K., Ali, A. M., & Cui, T. (2025). Human–AI collaboration in knowledge ecosystems: A multidisciplinary review, integrative framework and future directions. Journal of Knowledge Management. https://doi.org/10.1108/JKM-03-2025-0431

Author accepted manuscript, 38 PDF pages; first-page citation gives provisional journal pages 1–22. Locators use the supplied manuscript.

Audit: [ingestion record](../raw/ali-human-ai-knowledge-ecosystems-2025/log.md).

## Type

Systematic mapping and thematic synthesis of 101 Scopus articles; proposed integrative framework, not a meta-analysis.

## Key Insight

Collaboration depends on the surrounding organization as well as the user and model. This review supplies a map of factors to investigate, not a validated recipe for success.

## Key Findings

- Maps trust, model capabilities, organizational context and user expertise alongside explanation, task fit and governance (pp.12–19).
- Distinguishes possible gains in decision quality and engagement from risks including overdependence, opacity and unequal control of knowledge (pp.14–19,23–25).
- Calls for longitudinal and field research and for testing the framework in more varied institutional and cultural settings (pp.21–25).

## Key Passages

## Relevance

Adds organizational context to [[complementarity-framework]] without duplicating its concepts.

## Supports

- [[complementarity-framework]] — task, user and organizational conditions must be considered together.

## Contradicts / Extends

Broadens the unit of analysis beyond a person interacting with a model. Does not independently establish each causal mechanism described in its cited studies.

## Limitations

- Scopus-only, English-language journal scope; excludes conference proceedings and grey literature.
- Internal date inconsistency: methods state 2020–22 December 2024 (p.8), while discussion says 2014–2024 (p.17). Exact coverage should not be repeated as settled.
- The corpus includes reviews as well as primary studies; 101 articles is not 101 independent empirical tests.
- Framework links are synthesized propositions, not one jointly estimated causal model.
- Primary studies were not independently audited for this targeted contribution.

## Open Questions

- Which combinations improve joint task outcomes rather than only adoption or satisfaction?
- How do governance and task design affect expertise over time?
