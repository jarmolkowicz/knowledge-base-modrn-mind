## Summary
Li and colleagues preregistered a two-week study of 296 first-semester university students. Participants interacted daily with a supportive chatbot, a randomly assigned human peer, or a journaling control.

Only the human-peer condition reduced loneliness over time. Chatbot participants ended with loneliness similar to the journaling group, although both chatbot and human interaction reduced negative mood. The result separates immediate emotional relief from durable social connection.
