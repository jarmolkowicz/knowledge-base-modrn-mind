## Proposed Additions

### To Evidence and Rationale

**Related research, not validation:** Shen and colleagues randomized experienced Python users learning an unfamiliar library; the AI group scored lower on an immediate unaided quiz, while the main-study completion-time difference was not significant (PDF pp.6–9). Higher-scoring patterns involving conceptual questions were exploratory observations, not randomized prescriptions (pp.13–14). These results motivate checking what the learner can do separately, but do not prove that retaining one chosen step prevents skill loss.

### Evidence and limits

Bastani’s teacher-configured Tutor package produced no detectable same-session unaided exam difference from control (p.4). Fan’s abstract reports improved essay scores without significant differences in knowledge gain or transfer (pp.1–2; abstract-level check only in this pass). These are different tasks and supports, not one universal effect. None validates this card’s one-step boundary or establishes durable capability.
