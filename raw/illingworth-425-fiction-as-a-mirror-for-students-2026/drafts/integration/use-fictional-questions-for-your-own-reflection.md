# Use fictional questions for your own reflection

## Use When

You have read a work and want to connect its questions to your own experience.

Author’s intended benefit: Use fiction to prompt independent reflective writing before group discussion.

## Try It

1. Choose a character and an idea from the work. Ask AI for one short paragraph in that character’s voice and three questions for you.
2. [Inference] Treat the response as a new interpretation and check any claims about the work against the text.
3. Write your own responses privately for five minutes, as the source suggests; do not ask AI to answer for you.
4. For a class, discuss after the individual writing. [Inference] Offer a fictional or non-personal response option and let students keep private material private.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Proposed classroom exercise with adult first-person demonstration.

**Observed or reported:** Peha reports personal reflection after a fictional-character prompt; no results from students using this exact classroom protocol.

**Related research:** No directly relevant evaluation of this routine was identified in this review. The practitioner account above supplies its rationale; the AI adaptation remains untested.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- A generated character voice is an interpretation, not the original work or the author’s intention.
- Private reflections need not be disclosed to the tool or classmates.
- Five minutes of writing does not establish long-term inquiry or learning gains.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice which question led you to something you could support from the text or your own experience. Emotional resonance does not authenticate an interpretation.
