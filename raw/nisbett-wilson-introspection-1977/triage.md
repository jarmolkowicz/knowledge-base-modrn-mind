## Summary
Nisbett and Wilson review experiments showing that people often lack direct introspective access to the higher-order processes producing their judgments and choices. When asked for explanations, they frequently draw on plausible causal theories rather than reporting the actual process.

Reports can still be accurate when influential cues are salient and the person's causal theory is correct. The contribution is therefore a boundary on self-report and metacognitive access, not a claim that people never understand their reasoning.
