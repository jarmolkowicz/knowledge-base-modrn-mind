# Macnamara & Maitra (2019) — Limits of Practice-Hour Explanations

## Citation

Macnamara, B. N., & Maitra, M. (2019). The role of deliberate practice in expert performance: revisiting Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993). *Royal Society Open Science, 6*, 190327. https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190327

## Type

Paper — peer-reviewed, preregistered replication with retrospective practice estimates.

## Key Insight

Practice mattered, but accumulated hours did not distinguish the best from the good violinists in this sample. Protecting practice is defensible; treating its duration as a complete explanation of expertise is not.

## Evidence

- 39 student violinists, 13 per skill group; faculty nominations identified best and good students at the Cleveland Institute of Music, with the less accomplished group recruited from an affiliated university. Recruitment took more than four years (PDF pp.4–6).
- Interviewers were blind to skill group; participants were not told the hypothesis or group structure. Retrospective annual estimates of practice alone were collected before separate teacher-designed-practice estimates. Weekly diaries checked current practice estimates, not lifetime recall (pp.3–7, 13).
- Accumulated practice alone to age 18 differed across all groups: eta-squared .26, 95% CI [.03, .44]. The best group averaged 8,224 hours; good students 9,844; less accomplished 4,558. Best versus good was not significant (p=.364); good versus less accomplished was (p=.005). Teacher-designed practice showed the same pattern and explained .23 of variance (pp.15–16).
- Current weekly estimates exceeded diary-recorded practice, with no detectable group difference in that bias. Teacher-designed practice did not explain more performance variance than practice alone (pp.13, 15, 17–18).

## Key Passages

> "To be clear, explaining 26% of performance variance is not an inconsequential amount."
> — Macnamara & Maitra, PDF p.16

> "Definitions of key theoretical terms must be consistent in order to accumulate evidence for or against a theory."
> — Macnamara & Maitra, PDF p.18

## Relevance

Replicates selected comparisons from [[ericsson-deliberate-practice-1993]]. It helps prevent a useful practice principle from becoming an unsupported claim that hours determine expertise. This study contains no AI intervention.

## Supports

- [[strategic-alternation]] — [Inference] practice should be protected and evaluated for learning, without treating time alone as sufficient evidence of improvement.
- [[desirable-difficulty]] — complementary background only; the study did not manipulate practice difficulty, retention or transfer.

## Contradicts / Extends

- Contradicts part of [[ericsson-deliberate-practice-1993]]: the complete ordering of elite skill groups by accumulated hours did not replicate. The original comparison reported 48% variance explained; the replication's three-group analysis found 26%, using a different analysis specification.
- This is not evidence that practice is unnecessary, that innate ability explains the remainder, or that teacher guidance has no causal benefit.

## Limitations

Small selected sample, retrospective self-report, observational skill groups and differences in recruitment and analysis from the original. Confidence intervals are broad; a nonsignificant best-versus-good comparison is not proof of equality. Group membership is a proxy for performance. The study cannot isolate the causal effect of an additional hour or the quality of that hour.

## Open Questions

- How do practice quality, feedback, prior ability and opportunity jointly predict development?
- Does the relationship change across expertise levels and domains?
