# AI Boundary-Spanning

## What It Is

The use of generative AI to let one person reason and produce across *functional expertise boundaries they do not personally hold* — for example, a commercial specialist generating technically sound proposals, or an R&D engineer generating commercially viable ones. AI acts as a boundary-spanning mechanism, supplying enough of the adjacent domain's framing that a single worker approximates the cross-functional coverage a mixed team would provide.

## Why It Matters

Organizations invest heavily in cross-functional teams precisely because expertise is siloed: the value of putting a commercial person and an R&D person together is that neither alone spans both domains. Dell'Acqua et al. (2026) found that AI collapsed that distinction — without AI, professionals proposed ideas aligned to their function; with AI, commercial and technical participants produced a *balanced* mix spanning both, with no quality penalty for the added breadth. Non-core-job employees working alone with AI reached performance levels comparable to teams that included a domain expert. This matters because it changes what "having the right expertise in the room" requires, and because it raises a sharp preservation question: is the worker *acquiring* cross-domain understanding, or merely *renting temporary access* to it through the model?

## Key Insight

Boundary-spanning is not the same as the [[leveling-effect]]. The leveling effect is about *skill level* — AI compressing the gap between weaker and stronger performers on the same task. Boundary-spanning is about *scope of domain* — one person covering expertise territory that isn't theirs. A worker can be highly skilled and still be unable to span a boundary they lack the domain knowledge for; AI addresses that specific gap. The benefit is real and measured, but its durability is unknown and is the concept's central tension: spanning a boundary in the moment (producing a balanced solution today) is not evidence of *crossing* it developmentally (understanding the other domain well enough to judge the AI's contribution). [Inference] Where the human cannot independently evaluate the borrowed expertise, boundary-spanning risks becoming [[borrowed-certainty]] — confident output in a domain the worker cannot actually vet — which is why AI-enabled breadth is most trustworthy when paired with at least one party who genuinely holds the domain, or with deliberate verification rather than acceptance.
