## Proposed Additions

### To "Key Insight" section

AI management does not uniformly suppress voice. Across three Chinese scenario experiments, Wang et al. (2026) found greater willingness to speak up to algorithmic leaders in cognitive tasks, with no corresponding advantage in emotional tasks. The leaders explicitly invited input; actual speech and influence over decisions were not measured. Perceived fairness and psychological safety were associated with the effect, but their proposed causal sequence was not experimentally isolated. This adds a conditional opportunity for expressing agency, not evidence that algorithmic leaders are objectively fairer. See [[wang-algorithmic-leadership-voice-2026]].
