# The meaning of meaning in life: Coherence, purpose and significance as the three facets of meaning

## Citation

Martela, F., & Steger, M. F. (2016). The meaning of meaning in life: Coherence, purpose and significance as the three facets of meaning. The Journal of Positive Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2015.1137623

46-page preprint titled The Three Meanings of Meaning in Life; cover supplies published title and DOI. Main conceptual body pp.1–33; locators use preprint PDF.

## Type

Theoretical conceptual analysis and narrative discussion of previous literature; no new empirical sample.

## Key Insight

Making sense of life, having direction and finding life worthwhile are related but distinct. A single meaning score or a clear goal can hide which experience is changing.

## Key Findings

- Coherence is comprehensibility; purpose is future-oriented direction; significance is evaluated worth. The authors distinguish these from the metaphysical question of the meaning of life. (PDF pp.5, 9–21)
- Purpose may provide significance, but significance need not depend on goal attainment. The proposed interconnections are mostly conceptual and speculative. (PDF pp.19–27)
- Potential sources of meaning—such as belonging, authenticity, accomplishment and positive affect—should not automatically be included in the definition of meaning itself. Presence and search are also distinct. (PDF pp.28–32)
- The authors call for separate operationalization and empirical tests of the three facets. This paper does not establish a validated measure or causal ordering. (PDF pp.24–33)

## Key Passages

> "Coherence is value-neutral and descriptive whereas purpose and significance are inherently evaluative and normative"
> — Martela and Steger (2016), PDF p.18

> "are at this moment mostly conceptual and speculative"
> — Martela and Steger (2016), PDF p.25

## Relevance

Provides [[meaning-facets]] as a vocabulary for careful AI-related claims, without replacing meaningful-work definitions with a life-wide taxonomy.

## Supports

- [[meaning-facets]] — distinction among coherence, purpose and significance.
- [[agency]] — value and direction should not be inferred from fluent sense-making alone.

## Contradicts / Extends

Separates meaning from happiness and achievement, complementing the existing Baumeister source. Does not contradict research on affective experiences of meaning simply because it distinguishes affect from the conceptual facets.

## Limitations

Conceptual synthesis, no new empirical validation, and several proposed relationships explicitly speculative. Cross-domain application to AI or work needs separate evidence.

## Open Questions

- Which facet is actually measured when a study claims that AI increases or decreases meaning?
