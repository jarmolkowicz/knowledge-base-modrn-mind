# Buijsman, Carter & Bermúdez (2025) — Autonomy by Design

## Citation

Buijsman, S., Carter, S. E., & Bermúdez, J.-P. (2025). Autonomy by Design: Preserving Human Autonomy in AI Decision-Support. *Philosophy & Technology, 38*, 97. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-025-00932-2

## Type

Paper — peer-reviewed philosophical analysis using published empirical examples. No new participants, experiment, systematic review, or tested intervention package.

## Key Insight

Domain-specific autonomy requires both competence to make decisions and values that the person can recognize and endorse as their own. Keeping a human choice at the end of a workflow is insufficient if the person cannot detect bad advice, maintain the relevant skills, or notice changes in their own values.

## Argument and Evidence

The authors distinguish three threats: missing failure signals during a decision (pp. 3–6), weakening existing skills or preventing new learning over time (pp. 6–8), and unrecognized shifts in values through repeated AI use (pp. 8–11). Their last pathway is a theoretical extension from shorter empirical studies of inherited bias and opinion shifts; enduring changes in personal values are not established here.

Four responses follow: supply reasons to doubt unreliable outputs; give people substantive independent tasks; maintain essential skills through practice; and support reflection on values with carefully placed friction and sensitivity to value differences (pp. 11–18). These proposals concern the whole work arrangement, including organizational permission to disagree with AI. Several measures still need empirical testing.

## Relevance

Sharpens [[agency]] by distinguishing competent action from ownership of the values that guide it. Supplies a design framework rather than another generic warning about delegation.

## Open Questions

- Which failure warnings improve decisions without causing distraction or excessive distrust?
- Do independent practice and reflective friction preserve autonomy over months?
- How can systems adapt to personal values without reinforcing bias or collecting intrusive data?
