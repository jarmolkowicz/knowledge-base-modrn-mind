# Meaning Facets

## What It Is

Martela and Steger (2016) distinguish three facets of subjective meaning in life: **coherence**, life making sense; **purpose**, goals and direction; and **significance**, life feeling valuable and worth living.

## Why It Matters

A clear explanation, an achievable goal and a worthwhile life are different things. [Inference] AI can help make a situation easier to describe or plan without establishing what matters to the person.

## Key Insight

Coherence concerns understanding. Purpose concerns direction and motivation. Significance concerns value. They may support one another, but one is not proof of the others: clarity need not establish worth, and significance need not depend on goal attainment.

The paper offers conceptual distinctions, not a newly validated three-factor scale or demonstrated causal sequence. Its proposed relationships between facets are research hypotheses. It also distinguishes the presence of meaning from searching for it, and meaning itself from potential sources such as belonging, authenticity or achievement (PDF pp.16–33).

## Evidence limits

This is about subjective meaning **in life**, not a solution to the philosophical meaning **of life** and not a work-only assessment. Applying it to AI-supported work is an inference. Coherence here is not the same as fluent prose, factual accuracy or the [[coherence-trap]]. Do not treat these facets as a clinical diagnostic or universal personal-development checklist.
