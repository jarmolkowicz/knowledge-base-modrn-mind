# Use a creative constraint, then make something yourself

## Use When

You are exploring a low-stakes creative question and want an unfamiliar starting point.

Author’s intended benefit: Generate unfamiliar associations for creative work.

## Try It

1. Ask AI for a deliberately wrong answer to a harmless question. Keep the fictional frame explicit.
2. Optionally ask for a fictional rationale or turn the answer into a short poem or headline.
3. Choose an association and use it to start your own story, image or thought.
4. Variants: 489 limits an answer to a few words, then a sentence or image; 490 requires an “I don’t know” response and explores what could be needed to know. Pick a variant deliberately.
5. [Inference] Keep factual research separate from this exercise and label invented material if it could be mistaken for information.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Playful proposed exercise with a short demonstration.

**Observed or reported:** Author found a poetic association in a deliberately wrong answer; no creativity outcome measure.

**Related research:** Doshi and Hauser’s short-story experiment separates ratings of individual outputs from diversity across outputs. It does not evaluate this practice or establish lasting creative skill. See [[doshi-hauser-creativity-diversity-2024]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Keep deliberate falsehood within clearly fictional, low-stakes play.
- A compelling explanation is not evidence.
- No demonstrated improvement in unaided creativity or collective diversity.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice what you added or rejected, not just what the model produced. Forced uncertainty is role-play, not calibrated knowledge.
