# Set creative criteria before generating options

## Use When

You need a name or creative direction and risk choosing whatever sounds impressive first.

Author’s intended benefit: Establish creative criteria, generate broadly, then choose with context.

## Try It

1. Write the positioning and a brief: what is being named, desired associations, things to avoid, related names and audience constraints.
2. Bring together a small group with varied relevant perspectives. Generate word associations and themes before judging candidates.
3. Create a shortlist and compare it against the agreed criteria; Jackson uses red, yellow and green judgments.
4. Keep several contenders while checking practical constraints such as pronunciation, domain and legal availability.
5. [Inference] AI may add options after you define the brief. Retain people’s contributions and apply the same criteria to both.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Detailed professional naming process.

**Observed or reported:** Describes client naming work and trade-offs; no comparative trial.

**Related research:** Doshi and Hauser’s short-story experiment separates ratings of individual outputs from diversity across outputs. It does not evaluate this practice or establish lasting creative skill. See [[doshi-hauser-creativity-diversity-2024]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Naming success depends on the product and execution as well as the name.
- AI adaptation is not in the source.
- Legal availability requires a separate competent check.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice whether criteria changed merely to favor the first attractive candidate. A long idea list is not a measure of creative judgment.
