# Contextual Transaction Cost

## What It Is

The cost of making task context usable as it passes between AI agents, memory, tools, and human reviewers. Liu (2026, pp.6–7) includes transfer and latency, handoffs, compression loss, changes in meaning, verification, and governance.

## Why It Matters

An additional agent can contribute useful work while also creating another explanation to read, claim to trace, and handoff to check. Counting agents or judging only the final answer misses that burden.

## Key Insight

The proposed design test is whether gains from specialization, parallel work, independent evidence, and verification exceed the extra context and compute costs. Shared evidence can reduce repeated reconstruction but can also preserve a mistake that every agent then inherits.

Evidence is preliminary: an author-designed simulation favored shared and adaptive coordination, whereas the small real-model demonstration favored a single agent on efficiency. The concept is a useful cost checklist, not a validated universal equation or proof that a particular architecture is best. [Inference] Teams can record time spent reconstructing context and checking evidence before treating additional agents as a productivity gain.
