## Proposed Additions

### To "Key Insight" section

Tang et al. (2023) extend this topic beyond companion chatbots. Across four studies (N=794), the three-wave field survey linked greater AI interaction with greater affiliation need and loneliness; it does not establish causation. Two three-day field experiments randomized instructions to increase versus avoid AI use and found higher affiliation need and loneliness in the higher-use conditions. An online randomized simulation also found these social-state differences, but its downstream outcomes were intentions and expected sleep difficulty, not observed later behavior. Positive indirect paths connected affiliation need with helping and loneliness with insomnia and, less consistently, alcohol consumption. These mediation estimates do not establish the causal mechanism or uniform increases in total outcomes; the alcohol pathway was nonsignificant in one field experiment. The 2021–2022 workplace systems are distinct from modern generative companions, not an observed stage of a common chatbot dose-response trajectory.
