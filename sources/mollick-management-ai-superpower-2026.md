# Mollick (2026) — Management as AI Superpower

## Citation

Mollick, E. (2026). Management as AI superpower: Thriving in a world of agentic AI. *One Useful Thing*, January 27, 2026.

**URL:** [oneusefulthing.org/p/management-as-ai-superpower](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/management-as-ai-superpower)

## Type

Article (Substack post, practitioner-academic; Mollick is Wharton)

## Key Insight

Mollick describes executive MBA students building startup prototypes over four days using AI tools. His interpretation is that domain knowledge and management skills helped students define tasks and evaluate output. This is an instructor's account, without a controlled comparison against AI-native students.

His delegation model compares the time to do a task yourself with the time to request, wait for and evaluate AI work, potentially over repeated attempts. The relevant estimates are human baseline time, probability of an acceptable result and AI process time (p.2).

Mollick recommends clearer instructions, better evaluation and explicit completion criteria. These are proposed management practices, not experimentally isolated causes of success. His claim that knowing what to ask for becomes scarce is a framing of agentic work, not proof that execution or human expertise has become unnecessary.

## Key Passages

> "The skills that are so often dismissed as 'soft' turned out to be the hard ones."
> — Mollick, [p.6]

> "Management has always assumed scarcity: you delegate because you can't do everything yourself, and because talent is limited and expensive. AI changes the equation. Now the 'talent' is abundant and cheap. What's scarce is knowing what to ask for."
> — Mollick, [p.6]

> "Software developers write Product Requirements Documents. Film directors hand off shot lists. Architects create design intent documents. The Marines use Five Paragraph Orders (situation, mission, execution, administration, command). Consultants scope engagements with detailed deliverable specs. All of these documents work remarkably well as AI prompts for this new world of agentic work… all of these are really the same thing: attempts to get what's in one person's head into someone else's actions."
> — Mollick, [p.5]

> "[D]eciding to delegate to AI depends on three variables: 1. Human Baseline Time… 2. Probability of Success… 3. AI Process Time… [Y]ou’re trading off “doing the whole task” (Human Baseline Time) against “paying the overhead cost” (AI Process Time), possibly multiple times until you get something acceptable."
> — Mollick, [p.2]

> "I suspect the people who thrive will be the ones who know what good looks like and can explain it clearly enough that even an AI can deliver it."
> — Mollick, [p.6]

## Relevance

Provides a practical way to consider delegation overhead, including the human work of checking a result. Its claims about expertise are practitioner interpretation, not controlled validation of protection against AI errors.

## Open Questions

- How well can people estimate success and evaluation time outside their expertise?
- Does the model predict time savings in longer or higher-stakes work?
- What effects does managing AI work have on meaning and skill? [[nikolova-robots-meaning-2024]] and [[passalacqua-less-ai-2024]] use different technologies and outcomes and cannot settle that question.
