## Summary

The study explores how critical events can change HR managers' interpretations of meaningful work. Participants' accounts suggest that reevaluating what matters can connect temporary episodes with a more enduring meaning framework.

This adds a dynamic account to job-attribute and snapshot approaches. Claims about lasting transformation remain retrospective interpretations, not prospective follow-up evidence or a tested intervention for identity threats.

## Relevance

- Risk: Indirect — conflict between professional values and role demands.
- Erosion: Reported meaning changes, not measured skills or AI impact.
- Preservation: Indirect — distinguish a difficult episode from a changed understanding of worthwhile work.
- **Relevance**: MEDIUM

## Quality

- Evidence basis: Retrospective narrative interviews with 16 HR managers, yielding 37 critical incidents.
- Originality: Compared with [[professional-identity-threat]], [[identity-threat-coping]], [[callari-meaningful-work-2025]] and Bailey/Madden's episodic accounts. Adds critical-incident re-evaluation, not an AI coping trial.
- Limits: Small occupationally specific sample, retrospective recall and interpretive coding. Do not treat 37 incidents as 37 independent participants or proof of enduring change.
- **Quality**: MEDIUM — provisional triage judgment, not a formal critique verdict.
