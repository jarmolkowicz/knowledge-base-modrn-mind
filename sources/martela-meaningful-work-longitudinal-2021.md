# Martela et al. (2021) — Autonomy, Contribution and Later Work Meaning

## Citation

Martela, F., Gómez, M., Unanue, W., Araya, S., Bravo, D., & Espejo, A. (2021). What makes work meaningful? Longitudinal evidence for the importance of autonomy and beneficence for meaningful work. Journal of Vocational Behavior, 131, 103631. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2021.103631

Locators refer to the 15-page published version. The 49-page preproof describes the same study.

## Type

Three-wave observational survey of Chilean university alumni in work, with three-month intervals; N = 631, 240 and 148 across waves. Latent cross-lagged models with likelihood-based missing-data handling.

## Key Insight

Autonomy and perceived positive contribution predicted later meaningful work in the main three-month-lag models. This is bounded longitudinal evidence, not a causal recipe or support for every proposed pathway.

## Key Findings

- Only 148 of the initial 631 answered all three surveys. Retained respondents were older; no significant attrition differences were found on the tested baseline psychological variables (p.5).
- The meaning measure adapted four Presence of Meaning in Life items to work; competence and beneficence were perceptions, not skill or objective beneficiary outcomes (pp.5–6).
- In the combined main model, autonomy and beneficence predicted later meaningful work after baseline and other-predictor adjustment. Competence and relatedness did not independently show the same prospective pattern (pp.6–9).
- The separate three-wave mediation hypothesis was not supported. In that model, the T1 beneficence-to-T3 meaning direct path and total indirect effect were nonsignificant (p.10).
- Some reciprocal or cross-need paths were exploratory; the authors label their explanations speculative. Do not make them confirmed mechanisms (p.11).

## Key Passages

> "The results thus do not support our mediation hypothesis, and therefore hypothesis H5b is rejected."
> — Martela et al. (2021), PDF p.10

> "Thus, causality cannot be demonstrated with this method"
> — Martela et al. (2021), PDF p.12

## Relevance

Adds time-separated evidence about work meaning and helps distinguish felt competence from contribution. [Inference] AI-supported work should be examined for retained choice and visible benefit to others, without assuming these guarantee meaning.

## Supports

- [[agency]] — non-AI evidence about experienced autonomy, not proof of actual decision authority.
- [[meaningful-work]] — main prospective associations and limits.

## Contradicts / Extends

Qualifies the Martela & Riekki (2018) cross-sectional four-pathway account: not all correlates were prospective predictors. The nonsignificant three-wave mediation also limits a broad 'beneficence always predicts later meaning' summary.

## Limitations

One relatively advantaged Chilean sample, heavy attrition, self-report and six-month total span. Missing-data assumptions and nonsignificant baseline differences do not rule out selection. Conventional cross-lagged models do not establish within-person causal effects. The mediation model's SRMR .127 exceeds the paper's stated acceptable threshold; do not describe all fit indices as good.

## Open Questions

- Do the main relationships replicate under stronger within-person or experimental designs?
- How do attrition and alternative work-meaning measures change the results?
