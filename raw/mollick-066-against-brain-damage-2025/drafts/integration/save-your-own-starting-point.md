# Save your own starting point

## Use When

You want AI to extend or critique your ideas while keeping a visible record of what you thought before its suggestions.

## Try It

1. Read or encounter enough of the actual material to form a tentative view. Record a sketch, outline, rough draft or a few ideas of your own.
2. For a short decision, try the book variant: state what you currently think and what you most need to know. Uncertainty is a valid starting point.
3. Ask AI to address a specific need: alternatives, an unclear point or an objection. Keep the original visible.
4. Choose what to revise and explain why. Keep, change or discard suggestions; if your own first pass is sufficient, do not prompt further.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis / observed or reported:** Mollick reports his writing routine; Cagan reports a change in practitioner advice. Illingworth proposes counting occasions when no AI answer was needed. None tests this consolidated routine.

**Related research:** Overlaps [[think-first]], already labeled an inference in the KB. [[bjork-desirable-difficulties-2011]] supports distinguishing generation opportunities from fluent performance, with prerequisites; it did not test this AI sequence.

**Untested:** Reduced anchoring, greater authorship, improved creative work and retained unaided ability. These should not be inferred from simply saving an initial note.

## Limits

The initial view can be wrong and can itself anchor later judgment. Keep it revisable. No fixed solo-work duration, forced struggle or ban on accommodations. Novices may need source material or instruction before an independent attempt is useful.
