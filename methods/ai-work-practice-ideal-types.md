# AI Work-Practice Ideal Types

## Overview

Callari and Puppione (2025) describe three orientations toward Microsoft 365 Copilot in one workplace trial. Use them to discuss what people want from AI, what they retain, and what support they need. They are descriptive ideal types, not a maturity score or a validated assessment.

## What It Is / How It Works

| Orientation | Role assigned to AI | What the worker values | Common support need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Efficiency-Seeking | Tool for routine tasks | Time saved while retaining independent decisions | Simple use cases, reliable setup, clear boundaries |
| Pragmatic Integrator | Assistant supplying starting points and ideas | Refining outputs through personal expertise | Practice checking outputs; preserve contact with colleagues |
| Collaborative Optimiser | Expert-like sparring partner | Challenge, dialogue, and deeper analysis | Iterative questioning, critical feedback, clear final authority |

The types were constructed from 357 open-ended survey responses. They summarize patterns and aspirations; they do not demonstrate actual AI expertise, employee skill, or movement through stages.

## What To Do

[Inference] A discussion procedure based on the authors' recommendation to let employees identify with orientations and tailor support:

1. Ask for one recent task: what was delegated, what was checked, and who made the final decision?
2. Compare that account with the three orientations. Permit mixed or changing patterns; do not force a fixed label.
3. Ask what made the work meaningful and what became harder, including effects on colleague contact.
4. Agree one specific change in support or workflow, such as simpler routine-task setup, output-checking practice, or deliberate counterarguments.
5. Revisit using another real task. Judge support by retained capability and useful outcomes, not movement toward the most intensive AI use.

## Why It Works

The qualitative study provides a vocabulary for differing employee experiences. It does not test whether using this discussion procedure improves performance, meaning, or skills.

## Strengths / Limitations

- Makes different expectations visible without treating cautious use as failure.
- Connects task choices with expertise, [[agency]], and relationships.
- Based on one organization's early trial; no predictive validity or intervention evidence.
- An expert-like teammate is a user's framing, not proof of system competence.

## When It Applies

For voluntary team discussions and tailoring AI learning support. Unsuitable for performance ratings, mandatory personality labels, or deciding who should be replaced.
