# Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)

## Evidence Limits

The foundational works are identified below. The AI application is a proposal; the cited review of classroom scaffolding does not test generative AI.

## What It Is

The distance between what a learner can do independently and what they can do with suitable guidance or collaboration. Vygotsky's definition (1978, p.86) is cited here through van de Pol et al. (2010) and Tsim and Gutoreva's [[tsim-gutoreva-scan-2025|SCAN paper]].

## Why It Matters

[Inference] ZPD offers a way to ask whether assistance supports learning or merely completes a task. Applying the framework to AI does not establish that scaffolding always accelerates learning or that substitution necessarily prevents capability development.

## Key Insight

A simplified representation used in SCAN:
- **Known to learner** - Can do independently
- **ZPD** - Tasks currently achievable with suitable support
- **Unknown to learner** - Cannot do even with help (yet)

[Inference] AI can produce outputs beyond a learner’s independent ability. Whether the interaction supports learning depends on what the learner does and later demonstrates without help; producing an answer is not by itself scaffolding.

Scaffolding is a related tutoring concept, not a synonym introduced by Vygotsky. Van de Pol et al. (2010) describe support adjusted to the learner, gradual withdrawal and transfer of responsibility. Their review found limited effectiveness research and measurement challenges; it does not establish a universal learning procedure.
