## Type

Narrative expert review of human–autonomy teaming research, not a new experiment or pooled effect estimate.

## Key Insight

A capable AI component does not guarantee a capable team. Communication, coordination and people's understanding of teammates can limit joint performance; the evidence is setting-specific.

## Key Findings

- The review distinguishes team composition, communication/coordination, trust, shared cognition and performance rather than treating them as one outcome (pp.2–3).
- One reviewed composition study manipulated the belief that a teammate was AI while using human partners. Its findings concern expectations, not actual AI behavior (p.2).
- Trust can change with experience and task-specific competence; the review describes exceptions to a general human-over-AI preference (p.3).
- Shared mental models are a proposed coordination mechanism, but the review says the cited human–AI work had not quantitatively linked them to team performance (p.3).
- Authors call for realistic tasks, clearer agent roles and interdisciplinary measurement (p.4).

## Contradicts / Extends

## Limitations

Narrative review with heterogeneous tasks, agents and comparison conditions. Some studies use simulated or purported AI. It does not establish a universal decline in team performance, a fixed trust trajectory or a validated shared-cognition intervention.

## Open Questions

- Which communication and coordination designs improve real workplace outcomes?
- How should shared understanding be measured when one party is an AI system?
