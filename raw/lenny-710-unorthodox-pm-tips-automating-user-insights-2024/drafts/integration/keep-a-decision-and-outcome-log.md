# Keep a decision and outcome log

## Use When

You want to learn from decisions without rewriting your memory after the result.

Author’s intended benefit: Create repeated opportunities to compare prior reasoning with later outcomes.

## Try It

1. Write a decision or prediction, your rationale and the information available at the time. It can concern your work or a public product decision.
2. Start small: Yien suggests one brief entry each week.
3. Set a calendar date to revisit the outcome.
4. Compare the original reasoning with what happened and, where available, the actual reasons behind another team’s choice.
5. [Inference] If AI contributes, preserve your initial judgment separately and label what changed after its input. Do not let a retrospective AI summary replace the original record.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** First-person repeated practice with one retrospective example.

**Observed or reported:** Reports learning from decision records; his Shopify example matched direction but missed some underlying reasons.

**Related research:** Guingrich, Mehta and Bhatt offer a conceptual account of [[belief-offloading]], not evidence that this routine prevents it. See [[guingrich-belief-offloading-2026]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- A market prediction log is not a replacement for doing the work; Yien states this explicitly.
- Outcomes depend on luck and unavailable facts, not only reasoning quality.
- AI-generated predictions would exercise a different skill from making one yourself.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice wrong reasons behind right predictions as well as wrong outcomes. Count uncertainty and missing information, not only wins.
