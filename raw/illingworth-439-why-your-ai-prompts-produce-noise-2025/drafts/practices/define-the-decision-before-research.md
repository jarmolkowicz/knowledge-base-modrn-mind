# Define the decision before asking AI to research

## Use When

You keep collecting broad briefings but cannot say what they should change.

Author’s intended benefit: Tie research to a decision and signals that could change it.

## Try It

1. State the decision the research must inform.
2. Ask AI to help clarify the smallest useful question, three to five signals that would change your decision, and what can be excluded. Answer these questions yourself.
3. Confirm the focused question and signals before research begins.
4. Ask for a short set of insights, their practical implications and uncertainties; then identify what is still missing for your context.
5. [Inference] Check decisive evidence against its sources and reopen the scope if the exclusions removed a serious alternative.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** Proposed framework with first-person before/after demonstration.

**Observed or reported:** Mehra describes a more actionable newsletter answer after narrowing the question; no outcome comparison.

**Related research:** Guingrich, Mehta and Bhatt offer a conceptual account of [[belief-offloading]], not evidence that this routine prevents it. See [[guingrich-belief-offloading-2026]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Narrowing too early can exclude disconfirming evidence.
- Weighted insights are generated priorities, not measured weights.
- A specific answer can still be false.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Record which piece of evidence changed a decision, or state that none did. Brevity and apparent actionability are separate from accuracy.
