## Summary

Kasser and Ryan distinguish intrinsic aspirations, such as growth, relationships and contribution, from extrinsic aspirations centered on money, fame and image. Their analyses concern the relative centrality of these goals and their associations with wellbeing and distress.

The studies do not show that earning money is harmful or that replacing external goals causes better wellbeing. The authors discuss alternative explanations and acknowledge self-report, cross-sectional and cultural limits.
