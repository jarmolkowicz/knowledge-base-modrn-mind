# The ethical implications of artificial intelligence (AI) for meaningful work

## Citation

Bankins, S., & Formosa, P. (2023). The ethical implications of artificial intelligence (AI) for meaningful work. Journal of Business Ethics, 185, 725–740. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10551-023-05339-7

Supplied published PDF, 17 pages including publisher terms; main argument PDF pp.1–14.

## Type

Conceptual synthesis and ethical analysis; no new participant study or tested intervention.

## Key Insight

The implications of AI for work meaning depend on the human work left or created, and on who receives the benefits and burdens. A label such as augmentation is not sufficient evidence of preserved meaning.

## Key Findings

- The framework distinguishes replacement, tending the machine and amplification. Tending includes complex managing and fragmented routine minding; a real job may combine paths. (PDF pp.5–6)
- Five dimensions structure the analysis: task integrity, skill cultivation and use, task significance, autonomy and belongingness. Their predicted changes depend on replacement tasks, control, beneficiary contact and the wider implementation. (PDF pp.3, 6–11)
- The ethical assessment uses beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, justice and explicability. Better outcomes for one group can coexist with burdens for another, and meaningfulness does not override every other ethical value. (PDF pp.4–5, 11–13)
- The paper calls for empirical tests of the proposed relationships and deployment paths. Its examples do not constitute a causal evaluation of the framework. (PDF pp.13–14)

## Contradicts / Extends

Connects work meaning to distribution, worker control and accountability. Complements, not replaces, the job-characteristics and autonomy-preserving design frameworks.

## Limitations

Theoretical synthesis with selected examples and predictions; no validated effect estimates, score or guaranteed benefit. Predictions about which worker groups gain can change across technologies and settings. Historical technology claims are not presented as current forecasts.

## Open Questions

- Which predicted changes occur in a specific workplace, and how are they distributed among workers?
