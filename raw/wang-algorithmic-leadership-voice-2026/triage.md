## Summary
Wang and colleagues use three scenario experiments with Chinese participants to compare willingness to speak up to algorithmic and human leaders. Participants report greater willingness to voice concerns to algorithmic leaders, but only for cognitive tasks rather than emotional ones.

Perceived fairness and psychological safety sequentially explain the effect: algorithmic leaders seem fairer, which makes speaking up feel safer. The studies measure stated intentions in scenarios, not actual workplace behavior.
