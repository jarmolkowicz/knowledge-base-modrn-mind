# Check what each person thinks was decided

## Use When

A meeting is ending and a tidy summary may conceal different understandings.

Author’s intended benefit: Make decisions, owners, timing and communication obligations explicit.

## Try It

1. Reserve time at the end. Ask participants what they think was decided; Cohn describes having people write their answers.
2. Compare the accounts and surface differences before declaring agreement.
3. Record what was decided, who does what by when, and who else needs to know.
4. Assign responsibility for follow-up and make correction part of the ritual.
5. [Inference] If AI produced meeting notes, compare them with the reconciled human record rather than letting them define the decision.

## Limits

- A visible summary can silence disagreement if it appears settled too early.
- AI adaptation is inferred, not evaluated in the interview.
- Being listed as owner does not establish actual agreement or authority.
