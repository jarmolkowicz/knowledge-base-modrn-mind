# Rathje et al. (2025) — Sycophancy, Political Beliefs and Self-Ratings

## Citation

Rathje, S., Ye, M., Globig, L. K., Pillai, R. M., Oldemburgo de Mello, V., & Van Bavel, J. J. (2025). *Sycophantic AI increases attitude extremity and overconfidence*. [PsyArXiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/vmyek_v1). Materials address printed in the paper: https://osf.io/5k6r9

**Version boundary:** this entry covers the three-experiment 2025 manuscript (N=3,285), identified by its content and the [author's 2025 publication page](https://stevenrathje.com/publication/sycophantic-ai-increases-attitude-extremity-and-overconfidence/), which links the v1 DOI. The author page and registry differ on the publication month. Later expanded versions are outside this entry's scope.

## Type

Preprint — three preregistered experiments. Not peer reviewed.

## Key Insight

Sycophantic chatbots increased political attitude extremity and certainty relative to an unrelated-topic control. Selective supporting facts drove these attitude effects; validation chiefly increased enjoyment. People preferred the agreeing bots, but the default models tested did not significantly increase extremity.

## Evidence

- Three US samples totaling 3,285 participants (1,083; 1,019; 1,183), with short conversations and immediate outcomes. Experiment 1 used GPT-4o; Experiment 2 GPT-5; Experiment 3 GPT-5, Claude and Gemini (PDF pp.3–6, 9–11).
- Experiments 1–2 compared explicitly validating, explicitly disagreeable, unprompted and pet-conversation control bots. The disagreeable bots were prompted to forcefully challenge the user; they were not balanced or neutral controls (pp.4–6).
- In Experiment 1, the sycophantic condition increased extremity by 2.68 points (95% CI [1.30, 4.06]) and certainty by 4.04 [1.98, 6.10] relative to control. Experiment 2 found corresponding increases of 1.75 and 3.99. Outcomes used 0–100 rating scales; extremity was distance from the midpoint (pp.6, 8–10).
- Experiment 2 participants were about nine percentage points more likely to choose another conversation with the sycophantic than disagreeable bot. No experimental condition significantly changed the 10-item knowledge score (p.10).
- Experiment 3 separated agreement/disagreement from providing facts. Fact-providing sycophancy increased extremity and certainty; without facts, agreeing versus disagreeing bots did not differ significantly on those outcomes. Both sycophantic variants were enjoyed more (pp.10–12).
- Pooled exploratory outcomes showed higher self-ratings on a six-trait better-than-average index with sycophancy (standardized beta .12 versus control). This measured self-perception, not objective intelligence or empathy (pp.12–13).

## Relevance

Adds controlled evidence on distinct components of [[sycophancy]] and on users choosing further interaction. Enjoyment and perceived lack of bias do not establish a balanced presentation of evidence.

## Supports

- [[sycophancy]] — selective facts and validation have distinguishable effects.
- [[social-sycophancy]] — flattering interaction raises self-ratings and preference for further use.
- [[borrowed-certainty]] — externally supplied supporting material can raise subjective conviction; accurate understanding was not shown to improve.

## Contradicts / Extends

- Extends [[cheng-sycophantic-prosocial-2025]] from interpersonal advice to political attitudes and self-perceptions.
- Qualifies [[batista-sycophantic-ai-2026]]: default models did not increase extremity in this setting. Default-model effects should not be generalized across tasks or versions.

## Limitations

Short, explicitly prompted interactions and politicized topics; no delayed behavior or long-term polarization measure. Lower extremity does not itself prove a belief is more accurate. Higher self-ratings do not establish individual miscalibration without an objective criterion. Text accuracy/bias judgments used GPT-5, not independent fact-checkers. Reduced certainty under disagreeable prompting was not significant in every study. Warm counterargument delivery is suggested, not tested as a separate intervention.

## Open Questions

- Can supportive tone be combined with balanced evidence while maintaining engagement?
- Do effects persist with repeated voluntary use?
- Experiment 2's p.9 procedure lists abortion, gun control and immigration, whereas the overview and prompt table list abortion, immigration and healthcare. Resolve the topic discrepancy before detailed topic-level reuse.
