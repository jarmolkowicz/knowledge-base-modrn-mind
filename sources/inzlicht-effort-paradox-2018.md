# Inzlicht et al. (2018) — Effort Is Both Costly and Valued

## Citation

Inzlicht, M., Shenhav, A., & Olivola, C. Y. (2018). The Effort Paradox: Effort Is Both Costly and Valued. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 22(4), 337–349. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.01.007

Supplied author manuscript, 24 PDF pages; manuscript page locators.

## Type

Narrative theoretical review of human and animal research; no new AI experiment or pooled estimate.

## Key Insight

Effort can be a cost and a source of value. Neither effortless output nor difficult work is inherently better.

## Key Findings

- Effort is applied intensity, not identical to task difficulty, motivation or time spent (p.3).
- Separate greater value attached to an effort-produced outcome from value attached to effort itself. The review finds more evidence for the first (pp.6–7).
- Valuation may be retrospective, concurrent or anticipated. Excessive demands, unrewarded effort and easy alternatives can change the pattern (pp.7–8).
- Learned industriousness is one proposed mechanism. Durable transfer and training remain research questions (pp.5, 9, 20).

## Key Passages

> "Nonetheless, there is more work supporting value for the products of effort than for the value of effort itself."
> — Inzlicht et al. (2018), PDF p.6

> "Although effort adds value, we are still learning about precisely when, why, and how this occurs."
> — Inzlicht et al. (2018), PDF p.8

## Relevance

Non-AI foundation for evaluating what is lost or retained when work becomes easier. [Inference] Ask whether delegated effort served learning, contribution or enjoyment instead of preserving difficulty for its own sake.

## Supports

- [[effort-paradox]] — distinguishes effort costs from product-centered and effort-centered value.
- [[effort-investment-review]] — value of retained effort, not a tested AI protocol.
- [[effort-heuristic]] — related but narrower valuation account.

## Contradicts / Extends

Does not establish the AI-specific loop currently described in [[effort-heuristic]] or the learning benefit of every difficulty in [[desirable-difficulty]].

## Limitations

Narrative synthesis across tasks and species, not a systematic effect estimate. Valuing effort is distinct from learning through it. Retrospective justification may increase subjective value without improving quality.

## Open Questions

- When does delegation remove burden versus valued involvement?
- Can effort-reward training produce lasting transfer in AI-supported work?
