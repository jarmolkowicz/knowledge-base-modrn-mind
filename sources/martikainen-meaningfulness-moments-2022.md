# Moments of Meaningfulness and Meaninglessness

## Citation

Martikainen, S.-J., Kudrna, L., & Dolan, P. (2022). Moments of Meaningfulness and Meaninglessness: A Qualitative Inquiry Into Affective Eudaimonia at Work. Group & Organization Management, 47(6), 1135–1180. https://doi.org/10.1177/10596011211047324

Supplied 46-page journal PDF; online copyright 2021, issue year 2022. Locators use PDF pages.

## Type

Qualitative study: 54 participants in four focus groups, recalling present or past work. Written accounts and discussion were analyzed for content, language and narrative structure.

## Key Insight

Recalled moments of meaningful and meaningless work differ from overall judgments about whether one's work is meaningful. The study sought feelings but also elicited evaluations.

## Key Findings

- Four themes organize the accounts: connection with others, contribution to others, conversion through personal growth, and confinement through restricted agency or wasted time. These are qualitative themes, not a validated assessment.
- Connection appeared in meaningful accounts and confinement in meaningless accounts in this dataset; contribution and conversion appeared in both positive and negative forms.
- Meaningless accounts used more emotionally vivid, experiential language but tended to be more static in narrative structure. Meaningful accounts included more evaluative language. These patterns do not diagnose an individual's capacity for reflection.
- The authors propose reflection, shared perspectives and control over time as possible responses. Their study does not test those interventions or establish that preventing meaninglessness is more effective than promoting meaningfulness.

## Key Passages

> "although we only asked about affective experiences, our answers contained evaluations, too."
> — Martikainen et al. (2022), PDF p.16

> "may introduce an element of evaluations."
> — Martikainen et al. (2022), PDF p.39

## Relevance

Adds a distinction between task-level recalled experience and global evaluation to discussions of AI work practices. AI itself was not studied.

## Supports

- [[ai-work-practice-ideal-types]] — prompts can separate a recent experience from an overall view of work.
- [[agency]] — confinement accounts include restricted control, without proving a causal mechanism.

## Contradicts / Extends

Meaninglessness is not assumed to be a simple low score on the same positive experience. Do not collapse emotional accounts, evaluation and adoption orientation into one measure.

## Limitations

Retrospective focus groups, not momentary sampling or a longitudinal design. Many participants were not currently in work; the London research-lab volunteer sample may be unusually psychologically sophisticated and educated. Prompts, group discussion and researcher interpretation shape the material. Theme counts are quotations, not prevalence among workers. No AI effects or tested remedies.

## Open Questions

- Would momentary sampling reproduce the distinction?
- How do task-level feelings relate to overall meaningful-work judgments during AI adoption?
