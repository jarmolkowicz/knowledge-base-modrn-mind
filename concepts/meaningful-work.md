# Meaningful Work

## What It Is

The experience or judgment that one's work has significance and is worth doing. It is not simply enjoyment, engagement, pay or productivity.

## Why It Matters

[Inference] AI can make a task faster without making the remaining job feel more worthwhile. Meaning needs its own evidence, rather than being inferred from output or adoption.

## Key Insight

Allan et al. (2019) distinguish meaningful episodes from a broader judgment about one's work. Bailey and Madden (2016), drawing on interviews with 135 people in ten UK occupations, describe meaningfulness as episodic and often recognized through reflection on events rather than continuously felt while doing a task (PDF pp.3–7). These qualitative accounts add detail about experience; they do not measure identity change or establish how often such moments occur. Allan's meta-analysis found strong associations with work attitudes but weaker associations with some performance-related outcomes. Those correlations do not show effects of an intervention.

Their model of meaningful work is not the only one: some measures assess a global judgment, others dimensions of meaningful experiences. State which is being measured. Do not equate a favorable job attribute with experienced meaning or a meaningful experience with permanent wellbeing.

Possible participant overlap between Bailey and Madden's 2016 and 2017 reports remains unresolved; do not count them as independent replications.
