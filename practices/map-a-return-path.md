# Map a return path before choosing

## Use When

A career or work choice feels irreversible and you need to distinguish actual constraints from assumptions.

Author’s intended benefit: Make practical reversibility and constraints explicit.

## Try It

1. Describe the choice, timeline and non-sensitive constraints.
2. Ask one question at a time about what returning would require in time, money and effort. Answer from your circumstances.
3. Map a possible route back and mark what is known, uncertain or assumed.
4. [Inference] Verify consequential external rules and costs with the relevant authoritative source or adviser. Decide yourself whether the remaining downside is acceptable.

## Evidence and Rationale

**Basis:** First-person career account plus proposed AI inquiry.

**Observed or reported:** Wang reports that naming the cost of returning reduced felt pressure during a career choice; the account does not isolate AI’s contribution.

**Related research:** Guingrich, Mehta and Bhatt offer a conceptual account of [[belief-offloading]], not evidence that this routine prevents it. See [[guingrich-belief-offloading-2026]]. This is context, not direct validation.

**Untested:** Whether this specific procedure improves judgment, independent capability, creative quality or calibration beyond the reported experience. A completed artifact, more options and felt clarity are different outcomes.

## Limits

- Visa, tax, pension and employment facts need authoritative checking; none is established by the prompt.
- AI must not reclassify a structural constraint as merely emotional.
- A return route can change after the decision.

## What to Notice

[Inference] Notice whether the return path became more specific, rather than merely more reassuring. Keep unresolved constraints visible.
