# Time reclaimed: temporality and the experience of meaningful work

## Citation

Bailey, C., & Madden, A. (2017). Time reclaimed: temporality and the experience of meaningful work. Work, Employment and Society, 31(1), 3–18. https://doi.org/10.1177/0950017015604100

Published issue dated 2017; copyright 2015. Supplied 16-page PDF, with printed pages 3–18. Locators use PDF pages.

## Type

Interpretive interview study: 44 workers in three occupations in south-east England (15 refuse collectors, 15 stonemasons, 14 academics).

## Key Insight

Participants often understood meaningful work retrospectively, connecting completed work with past practice and future contribution. These accounts do not imply that all meaningful work must be slow.

## Key Findings

- Narrative and emergent thematic analysis related meaningful experiences to shared reflection, control of working time and connections across past, present and future. Meaning was episodic in these accounts. (PDF pp.4–9, 11–14)
- Preferred pace differed: stonemasons valued slow craft work, while refuse collectors could value completing work quickly. Imposed pacing, wasted time and conflict with personal time appeared in accounts of meaninglessness. (PDF pp.6–13)
- Formal ceremonies and informal shared reflection could make a contribution visible. These are reported experiences, not tested interventions that reliably increase meaning. (PDF pp.7, 11–14)

## Key Passages

> "None of the three occupational groups regarded their work as consistently meaningful"
> — Bailey and Madden (2017), PDF p.11

> "We only draw on the experiences of workers in three occupational groups."
> — Bailey and Madden (2017), PDF p.14

## Relevance

Adds a temporal qualification to [[professional-identity-threat]]: meaning and occupational self-understanding can involve continuity, recognition and future contribution, not output alone.

## Supports

- [[meaningful-work]] — work-specific meaning; interpretation remains bounded by this source's design and limitations.

- [[professional-identity-threat]] — background on how workers interpret their contribution; no identity-threat effect measured.
- [[agency]] — experienced control over timing and pace, not a universal preference for slower work.

## Contradicts / Extends

Complements global meaningful-work ratings with accounts of meaningful moments. Sample overlap with Bailey and Madden (2016) has not been resolved; do not count the two as independent replications.

## Limitations

Small purposively selected interview sample, predominantly men and White participants, in three English occupations. Retrospective interpretation, not prevalence estimates, longitudinal observation or causal evidence. The paper does not study AI. Participants' views on safety procedures are not evidence that those procedures were unnecessary.
