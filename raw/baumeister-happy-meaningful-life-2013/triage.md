## Summary
Baumeister and colleagues distinguish happiness from meaningfulness using a three-wave survey of 397 US adults. The two are strongly related, but their predictors differ: satisfying needs relates more to happiness, while integrating past, present, and future, giving to others, identity, and self-expression relate more to meaning.

Meaning can therefore involve worry, effort, or discomfort rather than immediate positive feeling. This distinction is useful when judging whether AI improves felt ease while weakening purpose, contribution, or identity.
