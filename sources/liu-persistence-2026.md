# Liu et al. (2026) — AI Assistance Reduces Persistence

## Citation

Liu, G., Christian, B., Dumbalska, T., Bakker, M. A., & Dubey, R. (2026). AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance. *arXiv:2604.04721 [cs.AI]*, April 2026 (preprint, under review).

(CMU + Oxford + MIT + UCLA collaboration. Project page: https://ai-project-website.github.io/AI-assistance-reduces-persistence/)

## Type

Paper (3 preregistered randomized controlled experiments, N=1,222 total; mathematical reasoning + reading comprehension domains)

## Key Insight

Across three preregistered experiments (N=1,222), brief AI assistance improved assisted performance but reduced subsequent unassisted performance and persistence on mathematical-reasoning and reading-comprehension tasks. Participants exposed to AI were more likely to give up after it was removed (abstract, pp.1–2).

These are short-term effects after roughly 10–15 minutes of assistance, not evidence of lasting skill loss. The authors propose that rapid answers change expectations about task difficulty and time, and remove opportunities to practise persistence. Those mechanisms were not directly established; the discussion presents them as possible explanations (p.10). The stronger causal language in the abstract quotations below is the authors' interpretation.

## Key Passages

> "AI assistance improves performance in the short-term, [but] people perform significantly worse without AI and are more likely to give up. Notably, these effects emerge after only brief interactions with AI (∼10 minutes)."
> — Liu et al., [p.1, abstract]

> "Current AI systems are fundamentally short-sighted collaborators – optimized for providing instant and complete responses, without ever saying no (unless for safety reasons)."
> — Liu et al., [p.1]

> "We posit that persistence is reduced because AI conditions people to expect immediate answers, thereby denying them the experience of working through challenges on their own."
> — Liu et al., [p.1, abstract]

> "Good collaborators optimize for long-term objectives. ... a mentor encourages independent development by adjusting the type of help given and sometimes offering no help at all. ... they know when not to help."
> — Liu et al., [p.2]

> "After just ~10 minutes of AI-assisted problem-solving, people who lost access to the AI performed worse and gave up more frequently than those who never used it. ... if such effects accumulate with sustained AI use, current AI systems – optimized only for short-term helpfulness – risk eroding the very human capabilities they are meant to support."
> — Liu et al., [p.1, public significance statement]

## Relevance

Separates assisted output, later unassisted performance and persistence. Give-up behavior is a measured outcome; it is not itself a test of metacognitive monitoring or durable capability. The findings motivate testing assistance that supports longer-term learning, but do not validate a particular schedule or intervention.

## Open Questions

- Do the effects persist, fade or accumulate over repeated use?
- Do changed expectations about time and difficulty mediate giving up?
- Which assistance designs preserve persistence while improving task performance?
- Can metacognitive prompts or alternating assisted and independent work help? These are candidate interventions, not verified solutions from this study.
