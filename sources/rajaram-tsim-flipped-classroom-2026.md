# GenAI-Integrated Flipped Classroom Learning Design in Management Education

## Citation

Rajaram, K., & Tsim, F. (2026). GenAI-Integrated Flipped Classroom Learning Design in Management Education: A Conceptual Behavioral Stakeholder Framework. Supplied manuscript, September 5, 2026.

Supplied 54-page manuscript dated September 5, 2026. Journal publication and peer-review status not established from this copy.

## Type

Conceptual framework, not an empirical evaluation. Applies self-directed, self-regulated and social learning theories to pre-class and in-class relationships among educator, student and GenAI.

## Key Insight

The authors propose judging AI classroom design by whether it supports the learning processes each phase needs—not merely whether it supplies good content. The framework's learning benefits remain untested.

## Key Findings

- Two foundational propositions: AI should support rather than substitute for students' self-directed and self-regulated preparation, and support rather than displace social learning in class.
- Three proposed roles: educator as Designer-Conductor, student as Autonomous Co-Constructor, and GenAI as Dynamic Pedagogical Agent. These are design constructs, not observed or validated identity changes.
- The framework connects individual/collaborative preparation to mind maps, reflection journals and active application. Educators configure assistance, check feedback and decide when to intervene.
- Interaction logs are proposed as clues to reasoning, but the authors also warn that being observed can encourage a performance of competence. Logs are not direct access to understanding.
- The paper raises privacy and performative-reflection concerns and suggests protected journal portions. Its separate suggestion of non-transparent adaptive difficulty is an untested proposal, not evidence of learning benefits.
- Claims of stronger transfer, durable knowledge, professional judgment or the greater necessity of flipped classrooms are theoretical arguments here, not new measured outcomes.

## Key Passages

> "our framework (including two propositions) has not been examined empirically."
> — Rajaram & Tsim (2026), PDF p.39

> "This framework, we conjecture, offers a starting point rather than a conclusion."
> — Rajaram & Tsim (2026), PDF p.42

## Relevance

A limited conceptual extension to [[metacognition]] and educational judgment. Does not add experimental support to [[think-first]] or establish that any classroom sequence prevents skill erosion.

## Supports

- [[metacognition]] — learning design can distinguish preparation, self-assessment and later challenge.
- [[judgment-development-paradox]] — educator oversight and student practice remain design concerns, not tested outcomes in this manuscript.

## Contradicts / Extends

Moves beyond individual prompting to relationships across instructional phases. Treats AI's role as designed and supervised; do not infer human-like moral agency from the word stakeholder.

## Limitations

No empirical test, systematic comparative review or validated protocol. Assumes available educator expertise, technical capability and institutional support. Some categorical statements about AI's limits and pedagogical superiority exceed the evidence provided. Monitoring and private reflection create governance concerns. Apparent reasoning quality, subjective confidence, actual understanding and unaided transfer must remain separate outcomes.

## Open Questions

- Do proposed phase relationships improve independently tested learning or transfer?
- What workload and expertise does educator oversight require?
- Can transparent, consent-based monitoring help without encouraging performative reflection?
