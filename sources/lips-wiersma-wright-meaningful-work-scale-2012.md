# Measuring the meaning of meaningful work: Development and validation of the Comprehensive Meaningful Work Scale (CMWS)

## Citation

Lips-Wiersma, M., & Wright, S. (2012). Measuring the meaning of meaningful work: Development and validation of the Comprehensive Meaningful Work Scale (CMWS). Group & Organization Management, 37(5), 655–685. https://doi.org/10.1177/1059601112461578

Supplied 31-page published PDF; locators use PDF pages rather than printed journal pages.

## Type

Scale development grounded in earlier qualitative work, followed by pilot, exploratory and confirmatory employee samples in New Zealand and a retest subset.

## Key Insight

The authors test a multidimensional measure of meaningful work rather than a single overall score. A tested measurement model does not itself establish what causes meaning or whether an intervention works; the reported model fit also has an unresolved qualification below.

## Key Findings

- The development sequence used a pilot of 167 employees, exploratory sample of 405, confirmatory sample of 275 and two-month retest subset of 173. These are development stages, not four independent intervention trials. (PDF pp.11–18)
- The final 28-item CMWS has seven dimensions: developing the inner self, unity with others, service to others, expressing full potential, reality, inspiration and balancing tensions. Four content dimensions should not be mistaken for the complete seven-factor scale. (PDF pp.6–7, 17–24)
- The measure correlated with engagement, calling and other meaning measures; extrinsic motivation was not significantly associated. These constructs overlap but are not interchangeable. (PDF pp.15–16, 25)
- The pilot did not initially recover inner development and inspiration as intended. Items were revised using earlier qualitative material before further factor analysis. The final confirmatory model removed two additional items. (PDF pp.12–14, 17)

## Contradicts / Extends

Complements the broader concept of meaningful work with a specific measurement approach. The model's dimensions are not automatically equivalent to three basic needs or Martela's four proposed pathways.

## Limitations

Convenience recruitment, predominantly educated employees, self-report, iterative item/model selection and limited blue-collar representation. Strong engagement correlation warrants construct-separation scrutiny.

The printed confirmatory-factor-analysis results report chi-square 1148.38 with 370 degrees of freedom, CFI .972 and RMSEA .059 for 275 employees (PDF pp.16–17). The RMSEA does not reconcile with the reported chi-square, degrees of freedom and sample size under the usual unscaled calculation. These are the printed values; the discrepancy remains unresolved without further estimator or scaling details. Treat the strength of this model-fit evidence as uncertain. No corrected statistic, universal scale validity, intervention efficacy or misconduct claim is inferred.

## Open Questions

- Do the dimensions remain distinguishable in contemporary AI-supported work and across occupational groups?
