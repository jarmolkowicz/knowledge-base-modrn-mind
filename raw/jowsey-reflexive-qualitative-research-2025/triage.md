## Summary
Jowsey and four lead authors, supported by 419 qualitative researchers from 32 countries, argue that GenAI is methodologically incompatible with reflexive qualitative analysis. Such work depends on a positioned human researcher interpreting meaning, power, context, and lived experience rather than finding statistical patterns in text.

They reject GenAI use even for initial coding in these approaches and add environmental and labor-justice objections. The article offers a strong boundary case for work whose value lies in the researcher's situated judgment and authorship.
