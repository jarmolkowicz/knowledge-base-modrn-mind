# Acts of Kindness Practice

## Overview

A five-week activity protocol studied by Cregg and Cheavens (2023): do three acts that benefit others on each of two days per week. The trial found some advantages for perceived social connection over other self-directed activities. It did not study AI use.

## What It Is / How It Works

The source defines kind acts as small or large actions intended to benefit or please another person, typically costing the giver time or resources. The proposed mechanism is reduced public self-absorption: less attention to how others might be judging oneself. Evidence for this mechanism is suggestive rather than definitive.

## What To Do

To follow the activity schedule reported in the study (printed p. 930 [p.8]):

1. Select two days each week.
2. On each selected day, perform three acts intended to benefit other people.
3. Continue for five weeks.
4. The research protocol also included initial instruction, a two-week check-in, weekly questionnaires, and follow-up five weeks after practice ended. Personal use without this structure is an adaptation of the protocol.

[Inference] In an AI-use discussion, this could provide a concrete way to reserve time for action involving other people. Its effects on AI reliance or loneliness among chatbot users have not been tested.

## Why It Works

In the randomized trial (N = 122 adults with elevated symptoms), kindness produced greater social-connection improvement than social activities at week 5 (d = 0.32), and greater social connection than thought records at week 10 (d = 0.26). The other comparator/time combinations did not meet p < .05. Do not summarize the study as superiority over both comparisons at every time point.

## Strengths / Limitations

- Concrete schedule, active comparison conditions, and follow-up.
- Outcomes were self-reported; attrition differed by group and COVID restrictions affected some participants.
- The study did not compare kindness with full therapist-led treatment or establish benefits for recipients.
- A personal connection practice, not a substitute for needed mental-health care and not a validated intervention for AI dependence.

## When It Applies

As an optional activity for people interested in doing more for others. The study's symptom-screened sample does not establish universal effects or clinical suitability for every individual.
